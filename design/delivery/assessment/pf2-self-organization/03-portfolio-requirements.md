# PF 2 — Self-Organization: Portfolio Requirements

**Used when:** promotion review to P5/P6/P7, or lead/principal/architect-level hiring.

**Redesign note (2026-08-03):** every row below already required a real, shipped work product rather than a described intention — kept as-is. What's new: each item is now also the named evidence source for a badge (see `badges.md`) — a candidate clearing the "good" bar on a row here earns the corresponding badge, and it's recorded as such rather than only as a promotion-file entry. This revision also (a) rewrites the P5 "good" bar so both rows require a checkable, third-party-corroborated artifact instead of an unfalsifiable counterfactual, matching the manager/peer-corroboration standard already used at P2–P4 (`01-work-product-review-rubric.md`); and (b) splits the P6 "good" bar into two explicitly labeled, equally valid paths so it works for candidates embedded in a single client account, not only candidates with literal multi-team access.

## Required evidence items

| Item | Maps to PC | Quality bar: sufficient | Quality bar: good |
|---|---|---|---|
| A stakeholder expectation-setting example | 2.5 [P5] | A conversation or doc exists showing expectations set before the project started — for a client engagement, this is typically with the client PM/account manager and should reference what the SOW commits to | A specific, dated instance where a named third party (the client PM/account manager, or a teammate/stakeholder on internal work) confirms in writing what the situation was before the conversation and that the candidate's upfront framing changed the outcome — e.g. a documented scope/expectation the third party confirms would otherwise have caused a conflict or rework. The confirmation must be checkable (an email, a written note, or a short statement collected at review time), not the candidate's own account of a counterfactual. |
| Evidence of fostering team economic-thinking culture | 2.12 [P5] | One example of coaching a teammate through a cost/value decision | At least two dated instances, spaced apart, where a named teammate applied the cost/value framework on their own initiative without the candidate raising it in the moment — confirmed in writing by that teammate or by the candidate's manager observing the pattern, not asserted by the candidate alone. |
| A cross-team roadmap they managed | 2.6 [P6] | Roadmap doc plus one example of a cross-team blocker anticipated in advance (a roadmap spanning multiple concurrent client engagements/accounts counts) | Good bar is met via either of two equally valid paths, not a single fixed bar: **(a) intra-engagement/multi-workstream path** — the roadmap is used as the reference plan across teams/workstreams the candidate has access to, with blockers consistently caught early across all of them, backed by the §1.6 artifact (named contact + dated reference + what changed) in `badges.md`; **or (b) cross-account signal path** — per `badges.md` §1.8, the client attests in writing that the roadmap/framework was adopted as an account-level deliverable that raised confidence in the engagement. Path (b) is a distinct, equally valid route to the bar for candidates without multi-team access — it is not a weaker fallback used only when (a) is unavailable. |
| Evidence of fostering economic-thinking culture across several teams | 2.13 [P6] | One cross-team example of the practice being adopted | Same two-path structure as the row above: **(a)** a recurring practice adopted by several teams/workstreams the candidate has access to, not just modeled by the candidate, backed by the §1.6 artifact; **or (b)** per `badges.md` §1.8, the client attests the economic-thinking practice was cited as a factor in account confidence or continued engagement. Path (b) is equally valid, not a substitute of lesser weight. |
| (P7) An org-wide roadmap they managed, with expectations clarified across the org | 2.7 [P7] | Roadmap is documented and was communicated org-wide | Roadmap functions as the org's reference plan, with a documented case of it preventing a conflict at organization scale |
| (P7) Evidence of fostering an economic-thinking culture across the organization | 2.14 [P7] | One org-level example of the practice in use | The practice is visibly embedded in how multiple teams make decisions day to day |

## Submission format

- Link to roadmap docs, status records, or decision write-ups
- 1-paragraph context: the situation, who was involved, why the approach taken worked
- For the P5 rows: include the named third party's written confirmation as a separate attachment or quoted excerpt — not paraphrased by the candidate
- For P6/P7: name the teams/stakeholders involved and any friction encountered; state explicitly which path (intra-engagement or cross-account) is being claimed for each P6 row
- Where the underlying roadmap/status record lives inside a client's contracted environment and cannot be exported (NDA, data residency, IP terms), follow the in-place attestation path in `badges.md` §1.5 instead of exporting a redacted copy

## Review process

- Reviewed by a P6+ engineer not on the candidate's team, plus the candidate's manager
- 30-minute review session
- Questions to ask: "What's a commitment you had to renegotiate — how did that conversation go?" "Tell me about an economic tradeoff someone pushed back on — how did you resolve it?" "Walk me through the third party's confirmation — what would they say if we asked them directly?"
- **P7 only:** add a teaching demonstration — candidate teaches an economic-thinking framework or roadmap practice to a group; reviewer observes for clarity and evidence of adoption beyond the candidate's own team
