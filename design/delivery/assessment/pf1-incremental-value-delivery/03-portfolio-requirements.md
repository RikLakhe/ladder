# PF 1 — Incremental Value Delivery: Portfolio Requirements

**Used when:** promotion review to P5/P6/P7, or lead/principal/architect-level hiring.

**Redesign note (2026-08-03):** every row below already required a real, shipped work product rather than a described intention — kept as-is. What's new: each item is now also the named evidence source for a badge (see `badges.md`) — a candidate clearing the "good" bar on a row here earns the corresponding badge, and it's recorded as such rather than only as a promotion-file entry.

## Required evidence items

| Item | Maps to PC | Quality bar: sufficient | Quality bar: good |
|---|---|---|---|
| An epic/project breakdown doc they drove | 1.4 [P5] | Breakdown doc exists; the epic shipped | Doc shows scoped, prioritized pieces, confirmed team understanding, and a visible link to how priority/urgency was set |
| Evidence of fostering priority-setting culture aligned with org strategy | 1.10 [P5] | One example of aligning team priority to a stated org goal or, where applicable, an account/engagement strategy for a specific client | A recurring pattern across multiple cycles, with the link to strategy explicitly documented each time |
| A cross-team work breakdown they drove | 1.5 [P6] | Breakdown doc plus confirmation from another team's lead that it was understood (a cross-engagement breakdown spanning two client accounts, or two workstreams within one large engagement, both count) | Multiple teams adopted the plan, with at least one dependency conflict resolved proactively before it caused a delay |
| Evidence of fostering cross-team priority culture | 1.11 [P6] | One documented cross-team prioritization conversation | A recurring practice adopted by several teams, with dependencies tracked visibly across all of them |
| (P7) An org-wide initiative breakdown | 1.6 [P7] | Breakdown is documented and was communicated org-wide | Breakdown became the reference plan, with prioritization understood consistently across the organization |
| (P7) A preventative measure that stopped a recurring cross-team dependency issue | 1.12 [P7] | Measure documented and implemented at least once | Measure adopted org-wide, with before/after evidence the recurring issue actually stopped |
| (P7) Evidence of guiding the organization through risk, change, or uncertainty at org scope | 1.17 [P7] | One documented instance of guiding an org-scope response to risk/change/uncertainty | A pattern across more than one instance, with a clear before/after on how the organization handled it differently because of the candidate's guidance |

## Submission format

- Link to the breakdown doc, roadmap, or RFC
- 1-paragraph context: the initiative, the alternative sequencing considered, why this breakdown won
- For P6/P7: name the teams/stakeholders involved and any documented friction along the way
- Where the underlying breakdown/roadmap lives inside a client's contracted environment and cannot be exported (NDA, data residency, IP terms), follow the in-place attestation path in `badges.md` §1.5 instead of exporting a redacted copy

## Review process

- Reviewed by a P6+ engineer not on the candidate's team, plus the candidate's manager
- 30-minute review session: reviewer reads beforehand, session is for probing questions
- Questions to ask: "What did you get wrong in this breakdown, and how did you find out?" "How did you decide this was cross-team work rather than one team's job?"
- **P7 only:** add a teaching demonstration — candidate teaches the preventative measure or breakdown approach to a group; reviewer observes for clarity and evidence of adoption beyond the candidate's own team
