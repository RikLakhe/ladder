# Delivery — Autonomous Projects

3 open-ended projects, each mapping to a whole PF (or both) rather than a single sub-function — these are meant to be solved with real judgment, the way real work is. No step-by-step guide — acceptance criteria only. Each project is written so that, once complete, the project itself IS (or directly feeds) the evidence bundle/cycle/portfolio item a specific badge requires — see the "Badges this feeds" line on each and `05-badge-reference-card.md` for the full map.

Completing a project does not itself award a badge — the named instrument's Verifier still has to review the resulting artifact and sign off, per `../assessment/badges.md` §1.3, including the co-signer requirement at Tier 3 (P4) and Tier 5 (P6) where it applies.

## Project 1 — Own a ≥5-ticket initiative breakdown end-to-end

**[P3 target] with input from a manager or senior teammate / [P4 target] largely unaided**

**Maps to:** PF 1 — Incremental Value Delivery
**Badges this feeds:** DL-1-P3 (Dependency-Aware Sizer) if the resulting bundle meets the P3 rubric bar; DL-1-P4 (Unaided Resizer) is fed only in combination with a proctored live-demo pass — this project alone cannot earn DL-1-P4 (PF 1 has no P4 rubric/portfolio backstop, see `02-guided-exercises.md` Exercise 5's note)

**Brief:** Take a small multi-ticket initiative (several related tickets — not a full team-wide epic; see Project 3 for epic-level facilitation) and own its breakdown from start to finish — sizing, sequencing, dependency notes, and priority order — without someone else doing the breakdown for you. Pick, before starting, a real delivery unit (sprint, epic slice, or ramp period) that will naturally produce **≥5 tickets** — the badge's evidence bundle requirement is not optional polish, it's the acceptance bar.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- The initiative spans ≥5 real tickets from one identifiable delivery unit — not disconnected tickets pulled from unrelated periods
- Each ticket is sized so it can ship incrementally, not handled as one large undifferentiated block, with the sizing rationale documented in the ticket before work started
- Dependencies between tickets (and to other teams' or client teams' work, if any) are documented before work starts on them, verifiable by ticket timestamp
- At least one ticket's priority or dependency note is corrected unaided during planning, with the correction explained — the P4 bar (PC 1.9), not just a dependency noted once at P3
- At least one instance of resizing or reordering mid-initiative is documented, with the reason
- At least one ticket documents a decision made despite missing information, with a rationale written at the time (PC 1.13)

**What a good solution looks like:** see `../assessment/pf1-incremental-value-delivery/01-work-product-review-rubric.md` (P2/P3 bundle bar — this project's ticket set IS that bundle) and `02-live-demo-checklist.md` (P4 scenario, which must still be run live and separately to complete DL-1-P4).

## Project 2 — Run one full continuous delivery cycle with proactive status and a real cost/value call

**[P2 target] through [P4 target], depending on cycle depth achieved**

**Maps to:** PF 2 — Self-Organization
**Badges this feeds:** DL-2-P2 (Daily Progress Reporter), DL-2-P3 (Same-Day Escalator), DL-2-P4 (Early-Warning Communicator) — this project's acceptance criteria ARE the PF 2 rubric's continuous-cycle requirement, not a separate exercise leading up to it

**Brief:** Across one full, continuous 2–4 week sprint or delivery cycle — chosen and committed to before it starts, not selected retroactively — maintain proactive status communication throughout, and surface and resolve at least one real cost/value tradeoff that comes up in the work. This project does not tolerate assembling the "cycle" after the fact from scattered notes; the redesigned rubric specifically checks for a continuous, dated record.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- Daily or per-cycle status updates were proactive for the full 2–4 week span, not prompted, and verifiable against the channel's own dated history (not a summary written afterward)
- At least one committed task in the cycle was delivered with visible urgency
- Every blocker, delay, or cost concern that arose during the cycle was communicated the same day it was identified (P3 bar) — not just the first one
- For P4: at least one issue was communicated **before** it required escalation, with an explanation of what tipped you off early
- One documented cost/value decision within the same cycle, with an explicit rationale using a named framework or tradeoff, written at the time
- For P4: the same reasoning was shared with a teammate on their own decision, not just applied to your own work, within the same cycle

**What a good solution looks like:** see `../assessment/pf2-self-organization/01-work-product-review-rubric.md` (this project's record IS the evidence the rubric reviews) and `02-live-demo-checklist.md` (the retrospective walkthrough conversation, which requires manager/teammate corroboration of the same record — bring people who can confirm the timeline, not just your account of it).

## Project 3 — Facilitate a cross-functional breakdown or manage a small roadmap

**[P5 target — stretch assignment, real scope required]**

**Maps to:** PF 1 + PF 2 combined
**Badges this feeds:** DL-1-P5 (Epic Breakdown Driver), DL-2-P5 (Expectation-Setting Lead)

**Brief:** Either facilitate the breakdown of an epic or cross-team (or cross-engagement) initiative for a group (not just yourself), or take ownership of a small roadmap spanning several weeks of work, including stakeholder expectation-setting with an internal or client stakeholder.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- The group can restate the breakdown/roadmap back accurately after the session(s) you led
- At least one priority disagreement or ambiguous decision was surfaced and resolved (or explicitly deferred with a plan) during facilitation, not avoided
- Evidence exists of fostering a team priority-setting culture aligned to org/account strategy (PC 1.10) — a recurring pattern, not a one-off session, if aiming for the portfolio "good" bar rather than "sufficient"
- If roadmap-focused: expectations were clarified with stakeholders before the roadmap's timeline began, not renegotiated reactively after slippage — for a client-facing roadmap, this should trace to what the SOW commits to
- If roadmap-focused: evidence exists of fostering a team economic-thinking culture (PC 2.12), not just the candidate's own cost/value calls

**What a good solution looks like:** see the portfolio requirements docs in both `../assessment/pf1-incremental-value-delivery/03-portfolio-requirements.md` (items mapped to 1.4/1.10) and `../assessment/pf2-self-organization/03-portfolio-requirements.md` (items mapped to 2.5/2.12), plus the P5 live-demo/walkthrough scenarios in each PF's `02-live-demo-checklist.md`. This project is explicitly a P5 stretch assignment, so "good" here means real, reviewable evidence from actual shipped/facilitated work, not a polished writeup of a hypothetical.
