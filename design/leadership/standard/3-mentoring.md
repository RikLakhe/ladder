# Leadership — Mentoring

**Competency:** Leadership
**Primary Function:** 3 of 3 — Mentoring
**Domain classification:** Core
**Version date:** 2026-07-06

## Scope

Seeking out mentorship for one's own growth, and mentoring others — widening in scope from teammates to the whole organization, and eventually fostering mentoring as an organizational culture and developing other mentors. Applies P2–P7. Excludes the judgment/alignment/process content covered in PF 1, and the live-facilitation skill covered in PF 2.

## Performance Criteria by Level

**P2**
- A practitioner at this level can: seek out mentorship to grow their own experience

**P3**
- A practitioner at this level can: mentor teammates, sometimes, in an open, respectful, flexible, and empathetic manner

**P4**
- A practitioner at this level can: mentor teammates, reliably, in an open, respectful, flexible, and empathetic manner
- A practitioner at this level can: seek out mentoring opportunities specifically to create team redundancy and backfill ability
- A practitioner at this level can: mentor an engineer rotating onto an unfamiliar client codebase or stack, accelerating their ramp-up to productive, billable contribution

**P5**
- A practitioner at this level can: mentor teammates and members of other teams, as needed
- A practitioner at this level can: sustain a mentoring relationship despite utilization/billability pressure — protecting the (typically non-billable) time it takes, rather than letting it lapse whenever chargeable work is tight

**P6**
- A practitioner at this level can: mentor across teams
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of mentoring across teams by seeking out mentoring opportunities for themselves and others

**P7**
- A practitioner at this level can: mentor across the organization
- A practitioner at this level can: foster an organizational culture of mentoring
- A practitioner at this level can: support others in their growth as mentors

## Required Knowledge

1. **Mentoring versus managing distinction** (mentoring is developmental and non-evaluative; it doesn't carry the authority or stakes of a management relationship) — without this distinction, "mentors teammates" risks sliding into unsolicited management, especially once mentoring extends beyond one's own team.
2. **Psychological safety in mentoring relationships** (what "open, respectful, flexible, empathetic" actually requires in practice — named explicitly in the matrix at P3/P4) — the specific manner of mentoring matters as much as the fact of it.
3. **Succession and redundancy planning basics** (bus-factor reduction, backfill readiness) — needed concretely for the P4 delta, where mentoring gains a deliberate strategic purpose beyond general development.
4. **Coaching-the-coach** (helping someone else become a better mentor, not just being one yourself) — needed specifically for the P7 delta, "supports others in their growth as mentors," which is a distinct meta-skill from personal mentoring.
5. **Rapid context-transfer mentoring** (compressing what a mentee needs to know to become productive on an unfamiliar client codebase, stack, or domain into a short ramp-up window) — needed from P4 up, since engineers in a services model rotate across client engagements far more often than in a single-product org, and mentoring frequently means onboarding someone fast, not developing them slowly over a stable tenure.
6. **Mentoring under utilization pressure** (protecting mentoring time when it competes directly with billable/chargeable work, and making the case for it to a Staff Manager or account lead when needed) — needed from P5 up; a mentoring standard that doesn't name this pressure will read as naive to anyone who has managed a chargeable team.
7. **Culture-building mechanics** (how a personal habit becomes a team, cross-team, or organizational norm) — shared with the parallel Required Knowledge items in PF 1 and in Feedback/Communication & Collaboration; the P6/P7 "fosters a culture of mentoring" PCs are entirely about this skill.

## Required Skills

- Giving developmental (non-evaluative) feedback and guidance
- Identifying redundancy/backfill gaps and matching mentoring relationships to close them
- Building trust-based mentoring relationships outside one's immediate team (P5+)
- Coaching other mentors on their own mentoring practice (P7)
- Ramping a mentee onto an unfamiliar client codebase/stack quickly enough to reach billable productivity (P4+)
- Protecting and justifying non-billable mentoring time against utilization pressure (P5+)

## Evidence Guide

**Critical aspects (non-negotiable):**
- Mentoring relationships are evidenced by real, recurring interaction over time, not a one-off conversation or a claimed open-door policy
- The redundancy/backfill purpose (P4+) is evidenced by an actual capability transfer — someone else can now do something they couldn't before — not just time spent together
- Where mentoring involves onboarding onto a client codebase/stack (P4+), it is evidenced by the mentee reaching productive, billable contribution faster than they would have unassisted — not just general encouragement
- Sustained mentoring under utilization pressure (P5+) is evidenced by the relationship continuing through at least one period of high chargeable-work demand, not just during slack time
- Culture-fostering claims (P6+) are evidenced by other people mentoring as a result, not just the practitioner's own mentoring relationships

**Assessment methods by level:**
- P2–P4: structured conversation about a mentoring relationship (as mentee and/or mentor), cross-checked with the mentee's perspective where available
- P5–P6: portfolio (evidence of mentoring relationships and, at P6, a mentoring culture forming) + a mentee or peer reference
- P7: portfolio + a teaching/coaching-the-coach demonstration

**Work products that demonstrate this PF:** documented mentoring relationships (1:1 notes, growth plans), redundancy/backfill plans referencing a specific mentoring effort, evidence of a mentoring program or norm introduced.

## Hiring Signals

- **P2:** describes a mentor they've sought out and what they got out of the relationship.
- **P3:** describes an instance of mentoring a teammate informally.
- **P4:** describes a mentoring relationship they built specifically to reduce a bus-factor risk or build backfill capability, and what capability transferred as a result; describes helping someone ramp up on an unfamiliar client codebase or stack.
- **P5:** describes mentoring someone outside their immediate team, and how they adapted their approach for a less familiar working relationship.
- **P6/P7:** not assessed via live scenario — see Evidence Guide (portfolio + reference + teaching/coaching-the-coach demonstration at P7).

## Promotion Criteria

- **P2 → P3:** from seeking mentorship for oneself, to occasionally mentoring a teammate.
- **P3 → P4:** from occasional mentoring, to a reliable practice, plus a new deliberate purpose of building team redundancy and backfill ability.
- **P4 → P5:** from team-scoped mentoring, to mentoring members of other teams as needed — the redundancy/backfill purpose continues unchanged.
- **P5 → P6:** from mentoring across teams as needed, to mentoring across teams as a matter of course, plus fostering a mentoring culture by actively connecting others to mentoring opportunities.
- **P6 → P7:** from cross-team mentoring and culture, to mentoring across the organization, fostering an organization-wide mentoring culture, and specifically developing other people's mentoring skills.
