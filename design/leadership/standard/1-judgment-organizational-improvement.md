# Leadership — Judgment & Organizational Improvement

**Competency:** Leadership
**Primary Function:** 1 of 3 — Judgment & Organizational Improvement
**Domain classification:** Core
**Version date:** 2026-07-06

## Scope

Making and owning decisions with awareness of bias, driving alignment to organizational strategy and goals, and improving practices and processes — from explaining bias and accountability concepts at P2, up to owning organization-wide decisions, alignment, and continuous process improvement at P7. Applies P2–P7. Excludes the live-facilitation skill covered in PF 2 (Facilitation) and the mentoring relationship covered in PF 3 (Mentoring).

## Performance Criteria by Level

**P2**
- A practitioner at this level can: explain how biases affect decision making, when asked
- A practitioner at this level can: explain what accountability for decisions and outcomes means, when asked
- A practitioner at this level can: contribute to conversations based on organizational strategy and principles, when appropriate, staying strongly oriented towards goals
- A practitioner at this level can: explain their team's practices and processes, when asked

**P3**
- A practitioner at this level can: reflect on their own biases when making a real decision, striving for objectivity
- A practitioner at this level can: hold themselves accountable for their own decisions and outcomes
- A practitioner at this level can: initiate conversations based on organizational strategy and principles, when appropriate, staying strongly oriented towards goals
- A practitioner at this level can: discuss an improvement to team practices or processes with the team, sometimes

**P4**
- A practitioner at this level can: have conversations based on organizational strategy and principles to ensure team alignment
- A practitioner at this level can: ensure their team is continuously working towards their shared goals
- A practitioner at this level can: discuss improvements to team practices and processes with the team, regularly

**P5**
- A practitioner at this level can: raise awareness, within their team, of how biases impact decisions
- A practitioner at this level can: take ownership of decisions made in their team, helping teammates make clear decisions aligned with organizational goals
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture within their team of having conversations based on organizational strategy and principles to create alignment
- A practitioner at this level can: collaborate with others, sometimes, to improve organizational practices and processes
- A practitioner at this level can: identify where a client's existing practices or tooling constrain an improvement, and propose a change that improves quality within those constraints rather than requiring the client to adopt LFT's default practice

**P6**
- A practitioner at this level can: take ownership of decisions made across teams
- A practitioner at this level can: ensure accountability is practiced throughout those teams
- A practitioner at this level can: foster this culture (of strategy-grounded, alignment-creating conversation) across several teams
- A practitioner at this level can: ensure several teams are continuously working towards their goals
- A practitioner at this level can: discuss improvements to practices or processes that affect several teams, with appropriate parties
- A practitioner at this level can: drive implementation of a cross-team practice or process improvement
- A practitioner at this level can: drive a consistent engineering standard across multiple concurrent client engagements, adapting how it is introduced to each client's context, contract type, and existing tooling without diluting the standard itself

**P7**
- A practitioner at this level can: take ownership of decisions made in the engineering organization
- A practitioner at this level can: ensure accountability is practiced throughout the organization
- A practitioner at this level can: foster this culture across the organization
- A practitioner at this level can: ensure goals are understood and continuously worked towards across the organization
- A practitioner at this level can: take ownership and responsibility for organizational practices and processes and their continuous improvement
- A practitioner at this level can: set an organization-wide bar for engineering practice that holds up under scrutiny from enterprise clients' technical leadership, while accommodating legitimate per-account variation in what can be directly changed versus negotiated versus worked around

## Required Knowledge

1. **Cognitive bias literacy** (common decision biases — confirmation bias, anchoring, sunk-cost reasoning — and how to notice them in your own thinking) — the entire bias thread of Decision Making, from explaining bias at P2 to raising bias-awareness for a team at P5, requires actually knowing what biases look like, not just that they exist.
2. **Accountability frameworks** (owning an outcome versus owning only the effort put in) — without this distinction, "holds themselves accountable" collapses into "tried hard," which isn't the same thing.
3. **Organizational strategy literacy** (what LFT's actual stated strategy and goals are) — see `../../my-role.md` and `../../goals/company-okr-2026.md`; shared grounding with Strategic Impact's Required Knowledge item 2 — "conversations based on organizational strategy" require a real strategy to reference, not an assumed one.
4. **Change management and process-improvement methodology** (how to propose, pilot, and land a practice change without just complaining about the old one) — needed for the P6 delta where Process Thinking shifts from discussing improvements to driving implementation.
5. **Goal-setting and OKR mechanics** (how goals cascade and how to check whether work is actually tracking towards one) — needed specifically for the Driving Alignment thread's "ensure continuous work towards goals" language, which requires an actual method for checking progress, not just a felt sense of alignment.
6. **Culture-building mechanics** (how a personal habit becomes a team, cross-team, or organizational norm) — shared with the parallel Required Knowledge item in Feedback/Communication & Collaboration; the P5–P7 "fosters a culture" PCs across this PF's Decision Making and Driving Alignment threads are entirely about this skill.
7. **Client-constrained improvement navigation** (distinguishing what can be changed directly, what must be negotiated with the client, and what must simply be worked around given contract type, client governance, or the client's own tooling/process mandates) — needed for every level from P5 up, where "improving practices and processes" happens inside an externally-imposed boundary rather than on a blank internal canvas; without this, process-improvement PCs read as if the practitioner has free rein over a single internal org, which is not the reality of a staff-aug or fixed-bid engagement.

## Required Skills

- Structured decision-making and bias self-checking
- Writing and reviewing decision records (ADR-style)
- Strategy-to-team translation (connecting organizational goals to day-to-day priorities)
- Facilitating a process-improvement proposal from idea to (eventually) implementation
- Coaching others' decision-making and bias-awareness (P5+)
- Negotiating a practice or tooling improvement with a client stakeholder who controls the constraint, and finding a workable middle path when the client declines (P5+)
- Holding a consistent engineering bar across multiple, differently-constrained client engagements at once (P6+)

## Evidence Guide

**Critical aspects (non-negotiable):**
- Decisions and their outcomes are owned by the practitioner, not attributed elsewhere when they go wrong
- Bias-awareness or objectivity claims are evidenced by a concrete instance where it changed an approach, not a general philosophy about being fair-minded
- Process improvements are evidenced by a real, specific change discussed or implemented, not a general complaint about how things are done
- At P5+, where the client controls the process or tooling in question, the improvement is evidenced by a change actually landed within that constraint (e.g., a negotiated exception, a workaround that preserves quality) — not a proposal that assumes internal-org authority the practitioner doesn't have on that account

**Assessment methods by level:**
- P2–P4: structured conversation about a real decision, a bias they've noticed in themselves, and a process improvement they've discussed, cross-checked against a real artifact (a decision record, a written process proposal) where available
- P5–P6: portfolio (evidence of fostering a decision/alignment/process-improvement culture) + a manager/peer observation
- P7: portfolio + a teaching/culture demonstration

**Work products that demonstrate this PF:** decision records or ADRs, retrospective notes proposing process changes, documentation of an implemented process improvement, planning conversation notes that reference organizational strategy or goals.

## Hiring Signals

- **P2:** can name a decision-making bias and explain why it matters; can explain what "being accountable" for a decision means in their own words.
- **P3:** describes a real decision where they noticed and corrected for their own bias; describes a team practice they suggested improving.
- **P4:** describes a conversation they had connecting team work to organizational strategy; describes a team practice change they helped land.
- **P5:** describes helping a teammate make a clearer, more bias-aware decision; describes fostering a strategy-grounded conversation habit on their team; describes a real instance of improving a practice within a client-imposed constraint rather than assuming free rein.
- **P6/P7:** not assessed via live scenario — see Evidence Guide (portfolio + teaching/culture demonstration). At P6/P7, the portfolio should show the practitioner holding a consistent bar across more than one client engagement, not just one large internal org.

## Promotion Criteria

- **P2 → P3:** from explaining bias and accountability concepts, to actually reflecting on personal bias and holding oneself accountable for real decisions; from contributing to strategy conversations, to initiating them.
- **P3 → P4:** from initiating strategy conversations, to having them specifically to ensure team alignment and following through on continuous goal progress; from occasional process-improvement discussion, to regular.
- **P4 → P5:** from personal decision-making and team-level alignment conversations, to taking ownership of team decisions, raising team-wide bias awareness, fostering an alignment culture, and extending process-improvement collaboration beyond the team.
- **P5 → P6:** from team-level ownership and culture, to owning decisions across teams, ensuring accountability across them, fostering alignment culture across several teams, and driving cross-team process implementation, not just discussion.
- **P6 → P7:** from cross-team, to owning decisions, accountability, alignment culture, and continuous process improvement across the whole organization.
