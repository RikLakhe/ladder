# Leadership — Facilitation

**Competency:** Leadership
**Primary Function:** 2 of 3 — Facilitation
**Domain classification:** Core
**Version date:** 2026-07-06

## Scope

Facilitating discussions so that everyone is heard, outcomes tie to stated goals, and — at senior levels — cross-team and organization-wide discussions converge on decisions with buy-in. Not applicable at P2–P3 per the LFT matrix; first appears at P4. Applies P4–P7. Excludes the judgment/alignment/process content covered in PF 1, and the mentoring relationship covered in PF 3.

## Performance Criteria by Level

**P2–P3**
- Not applicable — the LFT matrix marks this row "n/a" at both levels. Do not assess this PF for P2/P3 practitioners.

**P4**
- A practitioner at this level can: facilitate discussions within their team, ensuring everyone has an opportunity to share their opinion and be heard, that discussion outcomes tie to stated goals, and that no one person dominates the conversation
- A practitioner at this level can: facilitate a discussion that includes client stakeholders (e.g., a ceremony, a working session), applying the same balanced-participation and goal-tied outcome standard even though they hold no formal authority over the client attendees

**P5**
- Same bar as P4 — the LFT matrix states "P5: See P4" verbatim. No new behavior is expected at this level; growth here shows as range and reliability, not a new tier.

**P6**
- A practitioner at this level can: facilitate discussions across teams, ensuring relevant parties are included
- A practitioner at this level can: guide discussions toward decisions, clarifying points and securing buy-in
- A practitioner at this level can: facilitate a discussion where LFT and client stakeholders disagree, navigating client authority dynamics (e.g., a client decision-maker in the room, competing client and LFT priorities) to reach a decision with genuine buy-in rather than a decision the client attendee simply outranks the room into

**P7**
- A practitioner at this level can: facilitate organization-wide discussions, applying the same principles
- A practitioner at this level can: facilitate discussions that span multiple client accounts (e.g., cross-account practice alignment, a shared delivery standard), applying the same principles despite having no single unifying authority over any given client's side of the room

## Required Knowledge

1. **Meeting facilitation techniques** (turn-taking, time-boxing, parking-lot for off-topic items) — the mechanical toolkit underneath "ensures everyone has an opportunity to share their opinion" and "ensures no one person dominates."
2. **Group decision-making methods** (consensus, consent, decider-model, and when each is appropriate) — needed specifically for the P6 delta, where facilitation must "guide discussions toward decisions" and "secure buy-in," not just ensure balanced airtime.
3. **Reading group dynamics** (spotting disengagement, dominance, or unspoken disagreement in real time) — a facilitator who can't read the room can't act on what this row asks for.
4. **Cross-team stakeholder mapping** (knowing who actually needs to be in a cross-team discussion) — needed for the P6 delta's "ensuring relevant parties are included," which assumes the facilitator already knows who "relevant" means in a given cross-team context.
5. **Facilitating across the LFT-client boundary** (running a ceremony or working session with client stakeholders in the room, reading and navigating client authority dynamics, and steering toward a decision without having organizational authority over the client side) — needed from P4 up, since a meaningful share of this PF's real-world exercise is not purely internal; a facilitation standard silent on this understates the actual job.

## Required Skills

- Running a balanced, goal-tied team discussion
- Time-boxing and steering discussions back on topic
- Synthesizing multiple viewpoints into a decision with stated buy-in
- Identifying and including the right stakeholders for a cross-team or org-wide discussion (P6+)
- Facilitating with client stakeholders present, without formal authority over them, while still reaching a goal-tied outcome (P4+)
- Navigating a facilitation where a client attendee outranks the room, without letting that collapse into a decision imposed rather than reached (P6+)

## Evidence Guide

**Critical aspects (non-negotiable):**
- Participation balance is evidenced in a real session — quieter voices were heard, no one person dominated — not just claimed afterward
- Discussion outcomes visibly tie back to a stated goal, not just to "we talked about it"
- At P6+, buy-in is evidenced by stated agreement or an explicit decision to disagree-and-commit, not assumed from silence
- Where client stakeholders are present, participation balance and outcome ownership are evidenced across both LFT and client attendees, not just the LFT side of the room; a facilitator who only manages LFT participants hasn't demonstrated this PF in its full services context

**Assessment methods by level:**
- P4–P5: a live or simulated facilitation session, observed directly
- P6: a live or simulated cross-team facilitation session, observed directly, plus a portfolio example of a facilitated decision that stuck
- P7: portfolio + a teaching/facilitation demonstration

**Work products that demonstrate this PF:** meeting notes showing balanced input and a clear decision, retrospective facilitation records, cross-team meeting outcomes with named stakeholders and a documented decision.

## Hiring Signals

- **P4:** describes a team discussion they facilitated, and a specific moment they drew out a quieter participant or reined in a dominant one; describes a discussion they facilitated with client stakeholders present and how they kept it balanced and goal-tied without formal authority over the client attendees.
- **P5:** same bar as P4 — look for range (multiple kinds of discussions facilitated) rather than a qualitatively different behavior.
- **P6/P7:** not assessed via live scenario at the hiring-screen stage — see Evidence Guide (live/simulated observation + portfolio + teaching demonstration at P7).

## Promotion Criteria

- **P4 → P5:** no new behavioral bar — the matrix explicitly holds P5 to the same standard as P4. Promotion here should be justified by other PFs or by breadth/consistency of facilitation, not a claimed new facilitation skill.
- **P5 → P6:** from team-scoped facilitation, to cross-team facilitation with deliberate stakeholder inclusion, plus a new skill of guiding discussions to a decision with secured buy-in — not just balanced airtime.
- **P6 → P7:** from cross-team, to organization-wide discussions, applying the same principles at the largest scope this competency reaches.
