# Leadership — Autonomous Projects

4 open-ended projects. No step-by-step guide — acceptance criteria only. See the linked Phase D assessment files for the rubric a "good" solution is measured against, and `05-badge-reference-card.md` for the exact badge each project's evidence feeds.

---

## Project 1 — Drive a real process improvement to implementation

**Maps to:** PF 1 — Judgment & Organizational Improvement (whole PF: bias/accountability threads implicit in how the change is owned, plus the full Process Thinking and Driving Alignment threads)
**Level labels:** [P5 target] within your own team / [P6 target] across teams / [P7 target] org-wide
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-1-P5` (the ≥3-of-4 non-client portfolio items, alongside Exercise 9 for the mandatory client-constraint item), `LEAD-1-P6` (implemented cross-team process change with before/after evidence, plus the multi-engagement standard item if the candidate spans concurrent accounts), `LEAD-1-P7` (org-owned process + the org-wide engineering bar item)

**Description:** Take a process or practice problem that's been discussed but not fixed, and drive it through to an actual, implemented change — not just another round of discussion.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- The change is actually implemented, not just proposed
- A before/after comparison exists showing whether it helped
- If cross-team: the improvement is adopted by more than one team, with documented buy-in from each, backed by the §1.6 adoption artifact (named contact, dated reference, what changed) — not just the candidate's own account of adoption
- If the problem sits (fully or partly) inside a client's own tooling, process, or governance: the change is one that was actually achievable given that constraint — a negotiated exception or a workaround that preserves quality — not a redesign that assumes authority over the client's process
- If org-wide (P7 target): the standard holds up across more than one client account, with per-account variation explicitly named and justified, not diluted into a lowest-common-denominator rule

**What a good solution looks like:** see `../assessment/pf1-judgment-organizational-improvement/03-portfolio-requirements.md`.

## Project 2 — Facilitate a real cross-team or high-stakes discussion end-to-end

**Maps to:** PF 2 — Facilitation (whole PF: team-level balanced facilitation as the foundation, scaling to cross-team/cross-account convergence)
**Level labels:** [P6 target — real scope required] / [P7 target] organization-wide or cross-account
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-2-P6` (live cross-team/cross-account scenario evidence plus the portfolio item on a decision that stuck), `LEAD-2-P7` (org-wide or multi-account discussion, plus the teaching/facilitation demonstration)

**Description:** Take ownership of facilitating a real, high-stakes discussion that spans more than your own team — a cross-team dependency conflict, a contentious technical decision, a planning disagreement with real consequences either way, or a session that includes client stakeholders where you hold no formal authority over the client side.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- The discussion genuinely spans more than your own team, not just your own team's meeting relabeled
- A specific decision was reached, with buy-in confirmed from all sides, not assumed from silence
- If client stakeholders were in the room: buy-in was confirmed from the client side too, not just the LFT side, and any instance of a client attendee outranking rather than converging the room is named honestly
- You can describe what you'd do differently if you ran it again
- If targeting P7: the discussion spans the organization or more than one client account, with the §1.6 adoption artifact (or the §1.8 services-signal alternative) backing any adoption claim

**What a good solution looks like:** see `../assessment/pf2-facilitation/03-portfolio-requirements.md` — this project is explicitly a P6/P7 stretch assignment, so "good" here means real, reviewable evidence from an actual situation, not a rehearsed scenario.

## Project 3 — Build and sustain a redundancy-focused mentoring relationship, then widen it

**Maps to:** PF 3 — Mentoring (whole PF: personal mentoring practice widening from team to cross-team to org, plus the deliberate redundancy/culture thread)
**Level labels:** [P4 target] with a teammate / [P5 target] with someone outside your team / [P6 target] across teams, plus connecting others / [P7 target] org-wide, plus fostering the culture and coaching another mentor
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-3-P4`, `LEAD-3-P5`, `LEAD-3-P6`, `LEAD-3-P7` (paired with Guided Exercise 14 specifically for `LEAD-3-P7`'s coaching-the-coach item, which this project's mentoring-relationship evidence does not by itself satisfy)

**Description:** Build a mentoring relationship with an explicit redundancy or backfill goal, sustain it long enough to see a real capability transfer, and — as scope widens with level — extend the practice across teams, connect others into mentoring relationships, and (at P7) foster mentoring as an organizational norm.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- The relationship runs for at least a few months, with a documented cadence (not one-off)
- A specific capability is identified upfront, and there's evidence the mentee can now do it independently
- The mentee's own account of the relationship is included, not just the mentor's description of it
- The relationship survives at least one real period of utilization/billability pressure without lapsing, or (if the mentee is rotating onto an unfamiliar client codebase/stack) shows a documented fast-ramp phase alongside the longer redundancy goal
- If targeting P6: ≥2 concurrent/sequential cross-team relationships, plus one documented instance of connecting someone else to a mentoring opportunity, with the §1.6 culture-fostering artifact (or §1.8 alternative) attached
- If targeting P7: an org-wide relationship or program participation, an org-level culture initiative, and — run alongside Guided Exercise 14, not in place of it — a coaching-the-coach instance with a before/after account of the coached mentor's independent effectiveness

**What a good solution looks like:** see `../assessment/pf3-mentoring/01-work-product-review-rubric.md` (P4 row) or `03-portfolio-requirements.md` for P5/P6/P7.

## Project 4 — (Not a trainable exercise) Prepare for a genuine account crisis, and document it honestly if one occurs

**Maps to:** PF 1 — Judgment & Organizational Improvement, the P7-distinct crisis thread (1.9, 1.10)
**Level labels:** [P7 target — situational, not schedulable]
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-1-P7-CRISIS`

**Why this isn't a real "project" like 1–3:** per the badge award rule (`../assessment/badges.md` §1.3, item 4), `LEAD-1-P7-CRISIS` explicitly cannot be earned through a constructed exercise or role-played scenario — it certifies taking organization-level decision ownership through a *genuine* account crisis (active termination threat, executive-escalated delivery failure, or comparable stakes), not a routine escalation relabeled with bigger words. There is no guided exercise or assignable project that manufactures this legitimately. This entry exists so the badge isn't silently missing a training unit — the "training" here is preparation and honest documentation, not rehearsal.

**What to actually do, if you're a P7 candidate:**
- Read `LEAD-1-P7-CRISIS`'s full row in `../assessment/badges.md` now, before any crisis happens, so you know what a reviewable record needs to contain: what threatened the account and when, the decision(s) you owned during it, and a named, independently-confirmed outcome.
- If a genuine crisis does occur on your watch, keep a dated, contemporaneous record as it unfolds — not a reconstruction written afterward from memory — and loop in your delivery/account manager early, since their independent confirmation is a required co-signature regardless of your tier.
- Do not treat a routine escalation as a substitute or try to inflate one to fit this badge — the completion bar explicitly requires the co-signer to confirm the crisis was real, not asserted by the candidate.
- If no genuine crisis has occurred in your tenure: that's normal, not a gap. `LEAD-1-P7` (the non-crisis P7 badge) is the complete, standalone P7 credential for this PF — this crisis badge is a distinct, additional achievement, not a P7 prerequisite.

**What a good solution looks like:** see `LEAD-1-P7-CRISIS`'s row in `../assessment/badges.md` and the crisis-item note in `../assessment/pf1-judgment-organizational-improvement/03-portfolio-requirements.md`.
