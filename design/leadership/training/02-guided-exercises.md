# Leadership — Guided Exercises

14 exercises: 5 for PF 1, 6 for PF 2, 3 for PF 3, ordered simplest to most complex within each PF. Each maps to at least one sub-function (see `../functional-analysis.md`) and states which badge(s) in `../assessment/badges.md` it builds evidence toward, plus which assessment instrument it rehearses. Exercises 7 and 8 (PF 1) were added after the original six to close a coverage gap on PF 1's accountability and strategy-conversation threads; Exercises 9–12 were added to close a services-context coverage gap (client-constrained process improvement, facilitating across the LFT-client boundary, and mentoring under rotation/utilization pressure). Exercises 13–14 were added in the 2026-08-03 badge redesign pass to close two gaps the badge catalog exposed that no prior exercise built: PF 2's mandatory ≥3-session bundle at P5 (`LEAD-2-P5`), and PF 3's coaching-the-coach requirement at P7 (`LEAD-3-P7`). They all appear at the end of this file in numbering order, but slot earlier in the sequence — see `00-learning-path.md` for where each exercise actually falls.

**Reminder from the badge catalog (§1.3):** completing a guided exercise never itself earns a badge. These exercises build the real evidence artifact a badge requires; a named Verifier (and co-signer, where the badge's row requires one) still has to review it against the instrument named below.

## Exercise 1 — Bias-check decision journal

**Maps to sub-functions:** 1.1, 1.2
**Target level:** P2–P3
**Time:** ongoing, 2 weeks
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-1-P2` (bias-explaining baseline), `LEAD-1-P3` (bias-reflection + outcome-ownership evidence)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf1.../02-live-demo-checklist.md` P2/P3 items (bias questions), cross-checked against `01-work-product-review-rubric.md`'s Decision-making & accountability row

**Goal:** Build the habit of noticing and correcting for your own bias in real decisions.

**Setup:** Keep a short journal for two weeks.

**Steps:**
1. For each non-trivial decision you make, jot down: what you decided, and one bias that might have influenced it (confirmation bias, anchoring, sunk cost, or another).
2. At least once, deliberately check the decision against that bias before finalizing it — and note whether it changed anything.
3. Review the journal with a manager or senior teammate at the end of two weeks.

**Expected output:** a two-week decision journal with at least one instance of a bias-check changing an outcome.

**Check:** the journal shows genuine reflection (specific decisions, specific biases named), not a generic "I try to be objective" statement.

## Exercise 2 — Process-improvement proposal practice

**Maps to sub-functions:** 1.21, 1.22
**Target level:** P3–P4
**Time:** 60 min
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-1-P3` (process-improvement discussed at least once), `LEAD-1-P4` (regular process-improvement discussion, as part of the P4 bundle alongside Exercise 8)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf1.../01-work-product-review-rubric.md` Process improvement row; `02-live-demo-checklist.md` P3/P4 process-improvement prompts

**Goal:** Move from noticing a process problem to proposing a specific fix.

**Setup:** Identify one team practice or process that's causing friction.

**Steps:**
1. Write a short proposal: what's not working, what you'd change, and how you'd know if it helped.
2. Bring it to the team (a retro, a planning meeting) for discussion.
3. Note the team's reaction and any changes to the proposal that came out of the discussion.

**Expected output:** a written process-improvement proposal and a record of the team discussion.

**Check:** the proposal names a specific, testable change, not just a complaint about the status quo.

## Exercise 3 — Balanced-participation facilitation practice

**Maps to sub-functions:** 2.1
**Target level:** P4
**Time:** 45 min
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-2-P4` (team-discussion half of the bundle — pair with Exercise 10 for the client-facing half where the role is client-facing)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf2.../02-live-demo-checklist.md` "P4/P5 — Team discussion facilitation" scenario

**Goal:** Practice actively balancing participation and tying outcomes to goals in a real meeting.

**Setup:** Facilitate a real team meeting (a retro, a planning session, a design discussion).

**Steps:**
1. Before the meeting, identify one quieter teammate you'll make a point of drawing out, and note the meeting's actual goal.
2. During the meeting, actively invite that person's input at least once, and manage any one person dominating the conversation.
3. End with an explicit statement tying the outcome back to the stated goal.

**Expected output:** a short post-meeting note on what you did and how it went.

**Check:** a participant confirms the quieter teammate's input was genuinely drawn out (not performative), and the outcome is traceable to the stated goal.

**Sequencing note:** this exercise produces one session. `LEAD-2-P4`'s bundle needs this one plus (where client-facing) Exercise 10's session — do not treat this single session as sufficient on its own if the role is client-facing.

## Exercise 4 — Decision-convergence facilitation practice

**Maps to sub-functions:** 2.2, 2.3
**Target level:** P6 stretch
**Time:** 45–60 min
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-2-P6` (live cross-team scenario evidence, plus rehearsal for the cross-account variant if the role spans client engagements)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf2.../02-live-demo-checklist.md` "P6 — Cross-team discussion facilitation" (and, where applicable, "P6 — Cross-account facilitation with a client decision-maker")

**Goal:** Practice guiding a cross-team discussion to an actual decision with real buy-in, not just balanced airtime.

**Setup:** Facilitate (or shadow and then debrief) a real cross-team discussion with a genuine disagreement — a shared dependency, a conflicting timeline, or a resource conflict.

**Steps:**
1. Before the discussion, identify who the relevant parties from each team actually are.
2. During the discussion, guide toward a specific decision rather than letting it end in "let's think about it."
3. Explicitly check for buy-in from both sides before closing — ask, don't assume.

**Expected output:** a short write-up of the discussion, the decision reached, and how buy-in was confirmed.

**Check:** buy-in is evidenced by something more than silence — a stated agreement, or an explicit disagree-and-commit.

**Note:** the write-up from this exercise is a candidate input to `LEAD-2-P6`'s portfolio item ("a real cross-team facilitated decision that stuck"), but the badge's completion bar also requires the §1.6 adoption artifact (named participant, dated reference, what changed) — a single write-up from this exercise is not sufficient by itself; see `05-badge-reference-card.md`.

## Exercise 5 — Mentorship-seeking + informal mentoring practice

**Maps to sub-functions:** 3.1, 3.2
**Target level:** P2–P3
**Time:** ongoing
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-3-P2` (mentorship-sought evidence), `LEAD-3-P3` (one documented mentoring instance)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf3.../02-live-demo-checklist.md` P2/P3 items; `01-work-product-review-rubric.md` Mentoring relationship row

**Goal:** Build both sides of the mentoring habit — seeking it, and (once ready) offering it.

**Setup:** Identify a mentor you'd like more input from, and a teammate who might benefit from yours.

**Steps:**
1. Have at least one substantive conversation with a mentor about a real challenge you're facing.
2. Look for one opportunity to informally mentor a teammate — helping them work through something, not just answering a factual question.

**Expected output:** short notes on both the mentorship-seeking conversation and the informal mentoring instance.

**Check:** both notes describe a specific, real exchange — not a general description of "I talk to people about stuff."

**If no teammate is available to mentor this cycle** (e.g., placed solo on a client team): the mentorship-seeking half still stands on its own for `LEAD-3-P2`; the mentoring-instance half is marked "blocked — assignment-limited" per §1.7 in `../assessment/badges.md`, not treated as a failed exercise.

## Exercise 6 — Redundancy-focused mentoring plan

**Maps to sub-functions:** 3.3, 3.7
**Target level:** P4
**Time:** 60 min
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-3-P4` (redundancy-purpose instance with a named capability transfer)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf3.../01-work-product-review-rubric.md` Redundancy/backfill purpose row; `02-live-demo-checklist.md` P4 redundancy prompt

**Goal:** Practice mentoring with a deliberate strategic purpose, not just general development.

**Setup:** Identify a piece of knowledge or capability that currently lives with only one or two people on your team (including possibly yourself).

**Steps:**
1. Identify a teammate who could build that capability, and propose a mentoring relationship specifically aimed at it.
2. Set a concrete goal for what they should be able to do independently by a specific date.
3. Check in partway through and note what's transferred so far.

**Expected output:** a short redundancy-mentoring plan and a mid-point progress note.

**Check:** the plan names a specific capability (not "learn more about the system"), and the progress note shows something the mentee can now do that they couldn't before.

## Exercise 7 — Decision post-mortem (accountability)

**Maps to sub-functions:** 1.4, 1.5
**Target level:** P2–P3
**Time:** 45–60 min
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-1-P2` (accountability-explaining baseline), `LEAD-1-P3` (outcome-ownership evidence, alongside Exercise 1's bias-reflection evidence)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf1.../02-live-demo-checklist.md` accountability items; `01-work-product-review-rubric.md` Decision-making & accountability row

**Goal:** Practice explaining, and then demonstrating, accountability for a real decision and its outcome.

**Setup:** Pick a real decision you made (or were closely involved in) whose outcome you already know — ideally one that didn't go entirely as planned.

**Steps:**
1. Write down what you decided, why, and what actually happened.
2. Name specifically what you would own if it had gone wrong — not what someone else would own, and not just "I'd try harder next time."
3. Identify one thing you'd do differently next time as a direct result of taking that ownership.
4. Walk a manager or senior teammate through it, and be ready to explain in your own words what "accountability for a decision and its outcome" means.

**Expected output:** a short written post-mortem naming the decision, the outcome, and what you specifically owned.

**Check:** the accountability described is specific to this decision (not a general "I take responsibility for my work" statement), and clearly distinguishes owning the outcome from owning only the effort put in.

## Exercise 8 — Strategy-alignment conversation practice

**Maps to sub-functions:** 1.11, 1.12, 1.13
**Target level:** P3–P4
**Time:** 45–60 min
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-1-P3` (initiated strategy-conversation evidence), `LEAD-1-P4` (documented conversation connecting strategy to team alignment specifically, as part of the P4 bundle)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf1.../02-live-demo-checklist.md` strategy-conversation items; `01-work-product-review-rubric.md` Strategy & goal alignment row

**Goal:** Practice initiating a real conversation that explicitly connects a piece of team work to organizational strategy or goals.

**Setup:** Read (or re-read) `../../my-role.md` and `../../goals/company-okr-2026.md` so you have a real strategy and set of goals to reference, not an assumed one. Pick a piece of current team work.

**Steps:**
1. Identify which stated organizational goal or strategic priority the work actually connects to — be specific, not "this helps the company" in general.
2. Initiate a conversation with your team (a planning meeting, a 1:1, a retro) that explicitly draws that connection, rather than waiting for someone else to bring it up.
3. If operating at P4: frame the conversation specifically around team alignment — check whether everyone in the room actually shares the same understanding of the connection, not just whether you stated it once. Pair this with the goal-tracking method item the P4 rubric also requires (a recurring check-in record, not a description of one) — this exercise alone does not produce that artifact; see the learning path for where to build it.

**Expected output:** a short note on the conversation — what goal or strategic priority you connected the work to, what you said, and how the team responded.

**Check:** the connection named is a specific, real strategic priority or goal traceable to `my-role.md` or `goals/company-okr-2026.md`, not a vague gesture at "the mission."

**Sequencing note:** `LEAD-1-P4`'s full bundle (per `../assessment/badges.md`) needs this conversation record, a separate goal-tracking method artifact, ≥2 dated process-improvement notes (Exercise 2, repeated), and a client-constraint framing note (Exercise 9's framing step, done informally at P4). None of those four pieces substitutes for another — collect all four before treating the P4 bundle as complete.

## Exercise 9 — Improving quality within a client's existing constraints

**Maps to sub-functions:** 1.21, 1.22 (P4 informal framing); 1.23 and the client-constrained-improvement PC (P5 full skill)
**Target level:** P5+ (a lighter, informal version of step 1 also supports the P4 rubric's client-constraint framing check)
**Time:** 60 min
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-1-P5` — **this exercise builds the mandatory, non-substitutable client-constraint item (item 5 of `pf1.../03-portfolio-requirements.md`'s P5 rows).** Per `../assessment/badges.md`, `LEAD-1-P5` cannot be awarded without this item regardless of how strong the other four portfolio items are — treat this exercise as required, not optional, for anyone targeting P5.
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf1.../03-portfolio-requirements.md` P5 row "Evidence of improving practice within a client-imposed constraint"; informally, `01-work-product-review-rubric.md`'s P4 "Client-constrained improvement" row

**Goal:** Practice improving practice or quality within a client-imposed constraint, instead of assuming free rein to redesign the process.

**Setup:** Identify a real quality gap on your current account where the root cause is (at least partly) the client's own existing tooling, process, or governance — something you can't simply replace.

**Steps:**
1. Write down what's not working, and specifically which parts are within your control, which would require client negotiation, and which are non-negotiable given the contract or client governance.
2. Propose (and, where possible, land) a change that improves quality within the non-negotiable parts — a workaround, an internal-only mitigation, or a negotiated exception — rather than a proposal that assumes you can just replace the client's process.
3. Note what you tried to negotiate, what the client agreed to (if anything), and what you had to work around instead.
4. **If targeting `LEAD-1-P5` specifically:** go further than a one-off — aim for the "good" bar in the portfolio requirements, where the improvement is adopted as a lasting practice on the engagement, and you can name what you deliberately didn't try to change because the client controlled it.

**Expected output:** a short write-up distinguishing "changed directly," "negotiated," and "worked around," with the actual outcome of each.

**Check:** the write-up doesn't collapse into a generic internal process-redesign proposal — it names the actual client-side constraint and shows a real decision to negotiate vs. work around it.

**If the record depends on artifacts inside a specific client's own tooling and can't be exported:** use the §1.5 in-place-attestation path in `../assessment/badges.md`; if you've since rotated off that client before the record could be reviewed, that's §1.5a (access-loss), routed to §1.7 (assignment-limited) — not an unearned badge.

## Exercise 10 — Facilitating with client stakeholders in the room

**Maps to sub-functions:** 2.1
**Target level:** P4
**Time:** 45 min
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-2-P4` (client-facing half of the bundle, required only where the candidate's role is client-facing) — this session requires the delivery/account manager as co-signer when submitted as evidence, per `../assessment/badges.md`
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf2.../02-live-demo-checklist.md` "P4/P5 — Client-facing facilitation" scenario

**Goal:** Practice the P4 facilitation bar in a session that includes client stakeholders, where you hold no formal authority over the client attendees.

**Setup:** Facilitate (or shadow and then debrief) a real ceremony or working session that includes client stakeholders — a sprint review, a joint planning session, a requirements workshop.

**Steps:**
1. Before the session, note which client attendees might dominate or defer based on rank, and what the session's actual goal is.
2. During the session, apply the same balanced-participation standard to client attendees as to LFT attendees — invite quieter client-side voices, and manage any one person (LFT or client) dominating.
3. End with an explicit statement tying the outcome to the stated goal, checked with both sides, not just the LFT side of the room.

**Expected output:** a short post-session note describing how you managed participation and outcome ownership across both LFT and client attendees.

**Check:** the note shows genuine attention to the client side of the room, not just the LFT participants — a note that never mentions how client attendees participated doesn't meet the bar.

**If your role has no client-facing component:** this exercise is not applicable — go straight to combining Exercise 3's session(s) with Exercise 13's bundle-building below.

## Exercise 11 — Fast-ramp mentoring onto a client codebase or stack

**Maps to sub-functions:** 3.3, 3.7 (and the P4 client-ramp-up PC)
**Target level:** P4
**Time:** 60 min
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-3-P4` (the client-ramp-up instance half of the bundle, where applicable)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf3.../01-work-product-review-rubric.md` "Client-context ramp-up (P4 only, where applicable)" row; `02-live-demo-checklist.md` P4 client-ramp-up prompt

**Goal:** Practice compressing a mentee's ramp-up on an unfamiliar client codebase or stack into a short window, rather than general slow-paced development.

**Setup:** Identify an engineer newly joining (or about to join) your account, unfamiliar with the client's codebase or stack.

**Steps:**
1. Identify the 3–5 things they most need to know to become productive quickly — not everything about the system, just what unblocks near-term, billable contribution.
2. Structure a short (days, not months) onboarding sequence around those, with a defined point at which they're expected to make a first real contribution.
3. Track how long it actually took them to reach productive contribution, and what you'd tighten next time.

**Expected output:** a short ramp-up plan and a note on actual time-to-productive-contribution.

**Check:** the plan is specific to the client codebase/stack and prioritized for speed to billable contribution, not a generic "learn the system" checklist.

## Exercise 12 — Protecting mentoring time under utilization pressure

**Maps to sub-functions:** 3.3 (and the P5 utilization-pressure PC)
**Target level:** P5+
**Time:** ongoing, at least one billing cycle
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-3-P5` (the "sustained mentoring under utilization pressure" portfolio item)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf3.../03-portfolio-requirements.md` P5 row "Evidence of sustaining mentoring under utilization pressure"

**Goal:** Practice sustaining a mentoring relationship through a real period of utilization or billability pressure, rather than letting it lapse.

**Setup:** Use an existing mentoring relationship (yours, or one you're supporting) during a period where chargeable work is tight.

**Steps:**
1. Before the pressure hits (or as soon as you notice it), name the mentoring time explicitly — block it, and if needed, make the case for it to your Staff Manager or account lead.
2. Continue the relationship through at least one cycle of high chargeable-work demand, noting any sessions that had to be shortened or rescheduled rather than dropped entirely.
3. Note what you'd do differently to protect the time next time.

**Expected output:** a short note describing the pressure encountered, how the time was protected (or not), and the outcome.

**Check:** the note shows a real instance of the relationship surviving actual utilization pressure — not a hypothetical account of how you'd handle it in principle.

**Sequencing note:** this exercise assumes an existing mentoring relationship is already in place (built via Exercise 6 or an equivalent real relationship) — don't start this exercise from zero with a brand-new mentee; the "sustained through pressure" claim requires a relationship that predates the pressure.

## Exercise 13 — Facilitation range bundle (the ≥3-session log)

**Maps to sub-functions:** 2.1 (range/reliability delta at P5 — the matrix's "P5: See P4" means growth here is repetition and variety, not a new skill)
**Target level:** P5
**Time:** across one full review cycle (typically 6–12 weeks); each logged session is a normal 30–60 min meeting, not extra work
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-2-P5` — **this is the only training unit that builds this badge's required evidence bundle.** Per `../assessment/badges.md` and `pf2.../01-work-product-review-rubric.md`, `LEAD-2-P5` requires **≥3 real facilitated sessions across one review cycle**, with at least one team-only session and (where the role permits) one client-facing session, each independently meeting the P4 rubric bar. A single strong session — even a great one — does not qualify; this badge certifies range and reliability, not a one-time peak.
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf2.../01-work-product-review-rubric.md`'s P5-specific "Artifacts to collect" line

**Goal:** Build the dated, ≥3-session bundle `LEAD-2-P5` requires, instead of relying on a single memorable facilitation.

**Setup:** Pick the review cycle window before you start logging (don't retroactively assemble sessions from memory afterward). Plan to facilitate at least 3 real sessions within it, deliberately including some variety (not three copies of the same standing meeting).

**Steps:**
1. At the start of the cycle, identify 3+ real, upcoming sessions you'll facilitate — aim for variety (e.g., a retro, a planning session, and, if your role is client-facing, one session with client stakeholders present).
2. Run each session applying the P4 bar from Exercise 3/Exercise 10: balanced participation, goal-tied outcome, no one person dominating (and, for the client-facing one, the same standard extended to client attendees).
3. Log each session immediately afterward — date, participants, what was drawn out or managed, and the goal-tied outcome — rather than reconstructing notes weeks later.
4. At the end of the cycle, review all logged sessions together: do they each independently clear the P4 bar, and does the set show real range rather than one format repeated three times?

**Expected output:** a dated log of ≥3 facilitated sessions across the cycle, each with its own P4-bar evidence, plus a short cycle-end note on the range covered.

**Check:** each session in the log independently passes the P4 criteria (no averaging a great session and a weak one into a "meets" overall) — see the rubric's pass bar note that no new criterion is introduced at P5, only reliability across repetition.

**Sequencing note:** this exercise depends on Exercise 3 (and, where applicable, Exercise 10) for the underlying single-session skill — don't start this bundle before at least one team-only session has already cleared the P4 bar on its own. If a client rotation cuts the bundle short before 3 sessions are collected, this is an assignment-limited case per §1.7, not a failed exercise — the partial log is preserved and counted toward the next cycle.

## Exercise 14 — Coaching another mentor's practice

**Maps to sub-functions:** 3.10 (coaching-the-coach)
**Target level:** P7
**Time:** ongoing across at least one mentoring cycle of the coached mentor's relationship (typically 6–12 weeks); direct coaching sessions run 30–45 min each
**Builds toward badges:** `LEAD-3-P7` — **this is the training unit for this badge's mandatory coaching-the-coach item.** Per `../assessment/badges.md`, `LEAD-3-P7`'s completion bar requires this item specifically "at good bar (independent effectiveness demonstrated, not just participation)" — the org-wide mentoring relationship and culture-initiative items elsewhere in the P7 portfolio do not substitute for it.
**Instrument this rehearses:** `pf3.../03-portfolio-requirements.md` P7 row "Evidence of supporting others in their growth as mentors"; the P7 teaching/coaching-the-coach demonstration named in that file's Review process section

**Goal:** Practice the distinct meta-skill of helping someone else become a better mentor — observing their mentoring practice and coaching it, not mentoring their mentee yourself.

**Setup:** Identify a real, existing mentor (someone already mentoring at least one person) whose mentoring practice you can observe and give feedback on — ideally someone whose mentoring you have some visibility into already (a P4/P5 engineer building their own mentoring habit, or a peer running a mentoring relationship you can shadow with consent).

**Steps:**
1. Get explicit consent from the mentor being coached (and, where relevant, awareness from the mentee) that you're observing the mentoring practice itself, not the mentee's work.
2. Observe (directly, or via a debrief with the mentor immediately after a real mentoring session) at least 2–3 real mentoring interactions over the cycle.
3. Give the mentor concrete, developmental feedback on their mentoring practice specifically — e.g., where they slid into managing instead of mentoring (concept note §10), where psychological safety broke down (concept note §11), or where they missed a redundancy-purpose opportunity (concept note §12) — not feedback on the mentee's technical work.
4. Track a before/after account: what changed in how the mentor mentors as a direct result of your coaching, confirmed by the mentor themselves or by their mentee independently noticing a difference.

**Expected output:** a short before/after account naming the specific coached mentor, what you observed, what you fed back, and what changed in their independent mentoring practice afterward.

**Check:** the account shows the *mentor's* growth as a mentor, not just a description of the mentee's growth (that's the mentor's evidence, not yours) — and shows the mentor now doing something independently they weren't doing before your coaching, not just having had a nice conversation with you about it.

**If no mentor is currently available to coach this cycle:** mark this "blocked — assignment-limited" per §1.7 in `../assessment/badges.md`, per the explicit note on `LEAD-3-P7` — do not manufacture a coaching relationship to fill the gap; the default resolution is a reassignment or an agreed alternate, tracked by the manager/theme owner.
