# Leadership — Badge Catalog

**Status:** active — pilot badge system, layered on top of the assessment instruments (rubrics, demo/structured-conversation checklists, portfolio requirements) in `pf1-judgment-organizational-improvement/`, `pf2-facilitation/`, and `pf3-mentoring/`.
**Scope:** this file documents (1) the **generic, reusable badge pattern** — copied verbatim, structure-only, from `competencies/technical-skill/assessment/badges.md` Part 1, the canonical pattern source — and (2) the **Leadership badge catalog** — the content instance of that pattern for this competency's 3 PFs.

Badges do not replace the assessment instruments in each PF's `01-work-product-review-rubric.md` (or `01-code-review-rubric.md`-slot equivalent), `02-live-demo-checklist.md` (or structured-conversation-checklist-slot equivalent), and `03-portfolio-requirements.md` — a badge is *awarded* when a candidate clears the bar those instruments already define. Badges exist to make "substantial, portfolio-worthy work" legible and portable: a badge is evidence a specific piece of real decision-making, facilitation, or mentoring work cleared a specific bar, not a record of attendance, a training completion, or a self-reported quiz score.

**Design note (2026-08-03):** Leadership evidence at a service-based IT company is structurally different from Technical Skill's PR/design-doc evidence in one respect and identical in five others. The difference: leadership evidence is relationship- and conversation-based (decision records, facilitated sessions, mentoring relationships), which makes the §1.6 "adoption" instrumentation and the §1.5/§1.5a confidentiality/access-loss paths *more* load-bearing here, not less — a mentoring relationship or a decision record is exactly the kind of evidence that lives inside a client's own environment and disappears when a rotation ends. The five identical facts, carried forward unchanged from the canonical pattern: NDA/confidentiality can block evidence extraction (§1.5), access-loss is distinct from confidentiality (§1.5a), assignment/staffing shape (not skill) can block a required evidence type (§1.7), permanently-siloed accounts are a real, non-transient role shape at P6/P7 (§1.6a), and services-relationship signal is a legitimate OR-alternative to internal-standards evidence at Tier 5/6 (§1.8). All six are wired into every Part 2 row below where they apply — not selectively, per the explicit lesson from the Feedback/Communication & Collaboration pilot's P2 gap.

**Update note (2026-08-03, second pass):** a six-persona review of `badges.md` and the PF1–PF3 instruments surfaced 10 findings, fixed in this pass: missing §1.7 fallbacks on single-occurrence evidence (LEAD-1-P2, LEAD-2-P5), an orphaned Certifies-line claim not actually tested by any instrument row (LEAD-1-P3), a pass-threshold that let a facilitation failure through on the exact claim the badge makes (LEAD-2-P4), a 100%-assignment-luck mandatory item with no exception path (LEAD-1-P5), a co-signer/§1.8-evidence-source collapse for siloed accounts (new clause in §1.6a), two durative Certifies-line claims backed only by single-instance evidence (LEAD-1-P6, LEAD-3-P6), a "genuine crisis" claim with no independent corroboration (LEAD-1-P7-CRISIS), a buried services-differentiator at P7 (LEAD-1-P7 evidence-path ordering), and a missing evidence path for one-off, non-recurring strategic acts (new LEAD-1-P7 OR-option). See the specific rows/clauses below; each carries its own inline note where the fix departs from the generic pattern.

## Part 1 — The Reusable Badge Pattern (structural, competency-agnostic)

Copied verbatim from `competencies/technical-skill/assessment/badges.md` Part 1, the canonical pattern source. Only Part 2 below (names, artifacts, thresholds) is Leadership-specific.

**Back-port flag (2026-08-03):** the new clause added to §1.6a below (co-signer / §1.8 evidence-source collapse) is a genuinely generic mechanism, not Leadership-specific — it applies to any competency where a Tier 5/6 co-signer and a §1.8 services-signal evidence source can be the same person on a siloed account. It should be back-ported into Technical Skill's `badges.md` (the canonical pattern source) and into Delivery's and Feedback/Communication & Collaboration's `badges.md` Part 1. This file does not edit those other files; flagging only.

### 1.1 Badge object — required fields

Every badge, in every competency, is defined by these seven fields. No badge is complete without all seven.

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Badge ID** | `<CompetencyCode>-<PF#>-<Level>` — e.g. `LEAD-1-P4`. Stable, referenced from IDPs and assessment files. Never reused for a different achievement even if a badge is retired. |
| **Name** | A short, specific, memorable label for the achievement (not the competency name restated). |
| **Certifies** | One sentence, in the same verb+object+qualifier grammar as the standard's PCs: the specific, real-world capability claim an employer/reviewer/client could rely on if this badge is on someone's record. Where the underlying evidence is a single dated instance rather than a sustained/ongoing pattern, the Certifies line states the demonstrated instance ("has driven X, evidenced at time of review") rather than a durative/standing claim ("ensures X is practiced," "fosters a culture of X") — unless the completion bar is raised to require multiple dated instances across time. |
| **Evidence required** | The exact artifact or artifact bundle a candidate must produce — named specifically enough that two different assessors would collect the same thing. Always points back to a named row/item in this PF's rubric, checklist, or portfolio requirements (never a new, uncosted requirement invented only for the badge). Where the badge's Certifies line depends on an adoption/spread claim, the artifact named here is the adoption-evidence record defined in §1.6, not a description of adoption. Where the badge sits at Tier 5 (P6) or Tier 6 (P7), the field may name a services-specific alternate evidence option per §1.8, alongside (never instead of) the internal-standards evidence. Where a badge's required evidence type is vulnerable to being cut short by a mid-rotation client change (a bundle, a continuity-dependent cycle record, or a cross-account claim) — or depends on a single real-world occurrence that may simply never arise inside a given review window (a strategy-referencing conversation, a client-imposed constraint, an opportunity to facilitate) — the field should cross-reference §1.7 (assignment-limited/N/A status) as the fallback, rather than leaving that gap to be discovered ad hoc at review time. |
| **Completion bar** | The rubric/checklist/portfolio threshold that must be met — stated as "meets [row] at [level] with no HIGH/RED gaps," not a vague quality adjective. Where a badge's Certifies line depends on one specific checklist/rubric item more than the others (the item most directly tied to the badge's core claim), that item is named as mandatory and non-substitutable, rather than left to blend into a generic "≥N of M items pass" threshold that could let the core claim fail while the badge still gets awarded. |
| **Verifier** | Who is authorized to sign off (role/level, never "self-certified"). Matches the "who can assess" line in the underlying instrument. For badges tied to a specific client/account work engagement at Tier 3 (P4) and Tier 5 (P6) — where a misrepresented work context is both plausible and hard for a purely technical reviewer to catch — the named technical verifier is joined by a **co-signer**: the engineer's direct delivery/account manager, who confirms the claimed work context and scope actually happened. The technical verifier certifies the competency; the co-signer certifies the story is real. See §1.6a for the specific case where the co-signer and a §1.8 evidence source would otherwise be the same person. |
| **Tier** | Which of the six generic tiers (§1.2) the badge belongs to — set by the level it's tied to, not by how the competency's PF happens to be named. |

### 1.2 Generic tiers (map 1:1 to LFT's P2–P7 scope column, reusable across competencies)

| Tier | Maps to ladder level(s) | What the tier certifies, generically |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Foundation** | P2 | Executes a well-defined unit of work correctly, with guidance, within a single task. |
| **Tier 2 — Practitioner** | P3 | Executes the same class of work unaided, within the scope of an epic. |
| **Tier 3 — Senior Practitioner** | P4 | Executes more complex/ambiguous work unaided, within team scope, to a production-ready bar. |
| **Tier 4 — Guide** | P5 | Facilitates, mentors, or drives a change at team scope, working with the team's stakeholders. |
| **Tier 5 — Multiplier** | P6 | Facilitates or converges practice across several teams. |
| **Tier 6 — Steward** | P7 | Sets and governs standards/strategy at organizational scope. |

(Not every competency's every PF will produce a badge at every tier — a PF that caps at P4 stops at Tier 3. Don't force higher tiers to fill out a table; see the methodology's level-scoping principle. Leadership's PF 2, Facilitation, is exactly this case: it is n/a at P2–P3, so it starts at Tier 3.)

### 1.3 Badge award rule (generic)

A badge is awarded when, and only when:
1. The named evidence artifact/bundle exists and is reviewable (not described secondhand) — or, where §1.5's alternate evidence path applies, an in-place attestation exists in its place.
2. The completion bar (the rubric/checklist/portfolio threshold) is met with **no HIGH/RED-severity gap** on any criterion the badge's "Certifies" line depends on, and specifically on any criterion the Completion bar names as mandatory/non-substitutable.
3. A verifier at the required role/level signs off, in writing, with the artifact link and sign-off date recorded — including the delivery/account-manager co-signer at Tier 3/Tier 5, where §1.1 requires one, and including the independent-interview step at §1.6a where the co-signer/§1.8-source collapse applies.
4. The evidence reflects **real work product**, not a constructed showcase exercise built solely to earn the badge — per the "substantial, portfolio-worthy work" design principle, badges attach to actual decision records, real facilitated sessions, and real mentoring relationships, never to a standalone quiz, a training completion, or a role-played exercise with no real stakes. (Live-demo/structured-conversation-checklist-anchored badges are the one exception structurally allowed to use a live/simulated session rather than found-in-the-wild work, because the standard itself specifies that assessment mode at that level — but even there, the session must be a real, full-length facilitation or conversation, not a multiple-choice check.)

### 1.4 Reuse note for future competencies

- Keep the seven-field badge object and the six-tier table exactly as structured above.
- Replace only: Badge IDs (new `<CompetencyCode>` prefix), Names, Certifies lines, Evidence required (pointing to that competency's own rubric/checklist/portfolio rows), and Verifier roles if they differ.
- Do not invent a different tier scheme per competency.
- Do not award a badge for anything not already a named row/item in that competency's assessment instruments.
- Carry §1.5–§1.8 below forward unchanged — they are generic mechanisms discovered through Technical Skill's persona reviews and confirmed again here, structural facts about running a badge system at a service-based IT company.
- **Back-port note convention:** if a future competency's badges.md surfaces a further generic, competency-agnostic refinement, flag it and back-port it into Technical Skill's `badges.md`, the canonical pattern source. The crisis-badge co-signer extension in Part 2 (LEAD-1-P7-CRISIS) is deliberately scoped as leadership-specific, not proposed as a generic change (see the note on that row). The §1.6a co-signer/§1.8-source-collapse clause added in this pass **is** proposed as generic — see the flag at the top of Part 1.

### 1.5 Evidence-extraction constraints (generic)

Real work evidence sometimes cannot leave a client's contracted environment (NDA, data residency, contractual IP terms). This is a constraint on the *channel* evidence travels through, not a reason to waive the bar, and not something the engineer should have to solve alone:

- **Default path:** redact/sanitize the artifact (client-identifying names, data, proprietary logic) before it leaves the client environment, per the engagement's data-handling policy.
- **Expediting sign-off is the sponsoring delivery lead/account manager's job, not the engineer's to chase alone.** They own getting redaction/export sign-off actioned promptly — a stalled sign-off is a delivery-lead follow-up item, not a badge blocker the engineer is expected to resolve solo.
- **Alternate evidence path — when redaction/export is contractually impossible for a required PF:** the verifier reviews the artifact *in place*, inside the client's own environment/tooling (screen share, on-site review, client-hosted access), and signs a written attestation recording what was reviewed, when, and against which rubric/checklist row. No diff, document, or data needs to leave the client system. The attestation substitutes for the exported artifact; it does not lower the completion bar.
- This path applies to any competency's badge, not just Technical Skill — reused as-is here.

### 1.5a Access-loss is distinct from confidentiality (generic)

§1.5 above addresses evidence that *exists but can't leave* the client's environment. A different, and more common, failure mode — for Leadership, this shows up specifically when a decision record, a facilitated-session note, or a mentoring-relationship log lives inside a client's own tooling — is that the engineer rotates off a client **before** the evidence could be extracted or reviewed in place at all: access is simply revoked as part of offboarding, with no confidentiality issue in play.

- This is an **access-loss** case, not a confidentiality case: there is no NDA/IP barrier being worked around, the evidence is just physically gone.
- Do not require the §1.5 in-place-attestation path here — the verifier can no longer review anything in place, because access no longer exists.
- Treat this via **§1.7 (assignment-limited status)** instead: the badge is marked "blocked — assignment-limited," not "not earned" and not "failed," and the manager/theme owner works a resolution per §1.7's terms.

### 1.6 Instrumenting "adoption" claims (generic)

Any completion bar or Certifies line that uses adoption/spread language ("adopted by other teams," "sustained use," "fosters a culture," "adopted by ≥N teams/accounts") must be backed by a concrete adoption-evidence artifact, not asserted from memory or a candidate's own account. The generic artifact is a 3-part record:
1. A **named contact** at the adopting team/account who can confirm the claim if asked.
2. A **dated reference** to where the adoption is visible (Slack thread, email, PR, ticket, meeting note, mentoring-relationship record).
3. **One line** describing what concretely changed as a result (a practice actually followed, a decision that stuck, a second mentoring relationship that started because of the candidate's introduction) — not "they liked it."

A badge whose Certifies line depends on adoption cannot be awarded on the strength of the candidate's own description of adoption alone — this artifact is the required evidence, named explicitly in that badge's "Evidence required" field.

### 1.6a Permanently-siloed roles: §1.8 is the expected path, not the fallback (generic)

§1.6 and §1.7 above are written against a rotation model: a candidate normally gets, or will eventually get, visibility into more than one team/account, and a gap in that visibility is either temporary (§1.7's assignment-limited status) or fillable by producing the §1.6 adoption artifact. That model doesn't fit every P6/P7 case.

Some P6/P7 candidates work a **steady-state, permanently-siloed role**: a single large account with a durable client-imposed wall (security clearance, contractual exclusivity, a dedicated long-term staffing model) that has never given them, and realistically never will give them, shared visibility into a second team/account. This is not a rotation gap waiting to close — it is the stable shape of the role.

- Do **not** default to treating §1.6's cross-team artifact as the primary expectation with §1.8's services-signal path as a fallback for these candidates — that setup structurally penalizes a role LFT itself staffed this way.
- Instead, for a candidate the manager/theme owner determines is genuinely permanently-siloed (not merely mid-rotation), **§1.8's services-signal path is the primary, expected route** to the P6/P7 badge, and the §1.6 cross-team artifact becomes the alternate/supplementary path if it happens to be available — the two paths swap which one is "expected" versus "alternate" for this candidate only.
- This determination (permanently-siloed vs. temporarily-siloed) is made by the manager/theme owner, recorded with the badge sign-off, and revisited if the candidate's staffing model changes.
- This does not lower the bar — the candidate still needs a real, corroborated services-signal artifact per §1.8 (named client contact, dated reference, concrete change), not a description of goodwill.
- **Co-signer / §1.8 evidence-source collapse (added 2026-08-03, generic — flagged for back-port, see Part 1 top note):** for a genuinely permanently-siloed account, the same delivery/account manager is often both the §1.8 evidence source (the person confirming the client-relationship signal itself) and the Tier 5/6 co-signer §1.1 requires (the person confirming the claimed work context is real). Used together unmodified, this collapses two supposedly independent checks into one single source. Where this collapse would otherwise occur: the technical verifier (the P6+ engineer) must **independently interview the cited client contact directly** — not rely solely on the co-signer's secondhand account of what the client contact said or would say — before the badge is awarded. This is a required verification step, documented at sign-off (who was contacted, when, what was confirmed, by whom), not an optional enhancement and not satisfied by the co-signer's attestation alone.

### 1.7 Assignment-limited badges (generic)

Some required evidence types depend on what an engineer's current client assignment happens to expose them to, not on their skill — no teammate is available to mentor because the candidate is placed solo on a client team; the client's contract or working model gives the candidate no cross-team facilitation opportunity; a client rotation cuts off a bundle or cycle-continuity requirement mid-way; or the specific real-world occurrence a piece of evidence depends on (a strategy-referencing conversation, a client-imposed constraint worth improving against) simply doesn't arise during the review window even though nothing about the candidate's role structurally blocks it. When a structural assignment constraint — or a one-off non-occurrence of this kind — prevents producing a required evidence type for more than one review cycle:
- The badge is marked **"blocked — assignment-limited"** in the person's record — distinct from both "not earned" (skill gap) and "failed" (attempted, didn't clear the bar). Where the missing item is a single conversation/occurrence rather than a structural blocker, it may instead be marked **N/A for this window** on the specific sub-item, without blocking the rest of the badge, per the specific badge rows in Part 2 that call this out.
- The manager or theme owner tracks this status and works either a reassignment that restores access to the evidence type, or an assignment-appropriate alternate evidence substitute agreed with the theme owner. The default resolution is never "go build a side project to manufacture evidence."
- This status is reviewed at the same cadence as the badge/IDP review, not left open indefinitely.
- **Mid-rotation client swaps specifically:** if a client rotation breaks bundle or cycle continuity before required evidence is complete — the sprint/ramp period or delivery cycle the candidate was building evidence against ends early because they were moved to a new account — this is an assignment-limited case, not an unearned badge. Whatever partial record exists is preserved and counted toward the next cycle's evidence rather than discarded.

### 1.8 Services-company impact signal (generic, Tier 5/Tier 6 only)

At Tier 5 (P6) and Tier 6 (P7), pure internal-standards evidence ("adopted by internal teams," "org standard set") is not the only legitimate signal of multiplier/steward-level impact at a services company — impact can also show up in the account/client relationship itself. Where natural for the PF, the badge's "Evidence required" field lists a second, equally valid evidence option, such as: retained/expanded technical credibility on an account, a pre-sales/solutioning contribution that won or grew a client relationship, or external technical reputation (talks, publications, community standing) that visibly contributed to client trust. This is always an **OR** alternative alongside the internal-standards evidence, never a replacement for it — a candidate can clear the badge via either path. (See §1.6a for the permanently-siloed-role case where this path becomes the *expected* route rather than the alternate. See LEAD-1-P7 in Part 2 for a case where, for services-company reasons specific to that badge, the services-signal path is additionally named as *preferred*, not just equally valid, while still remaining a true OR — see the note on that row.)

## Part 3 — How badges are administered

- **Recording:** badge awards are recorded against the person's file (`people/<name>.md`) and/or IDP (`1-1s/<name>-idp.md`) with Badge ID, date, artifact link, and verifier — never inside this catalog file, which stays a reference, not a per-person ledger.
- **No self-certification:** every badge requires the Verifier role/level named in its row; a candidate cannot award their own badge.
- **Verifier co-signing at Tier 3 (P4) / Tier 5 (P6):** per §1.1, badges at these tiers require both a technical verifier and the candidate's delivery/account manager as co-signer wherever the evidence involves a real client-facing session, account, or engagement context. The technical verifier certifies the leadership competency demonstrated; the co-signer confirms the claimed work context and scope actually happened. **Where the co-signer would also be the §1.8 services-signal evidence source (typically on a permanently-siloed account) — see §1.6a — the technical verifier must additionally interview the cited client contact directly, independent of the co-signer's account, before sign-off.** LEAD-1-P7-CRISIS is a deliberate, leadership-specific exception: it requires the co-signer regardless of tier, because a purely technical P7/VP-level verifier cannot independently confirm that an account crisis was real or that the attributed outcome occurred — see the note on that row in Part 2. This exception is not proposed for back-port into the generic §1.1 rule; the §1.6a co-signer/§1.8-source-collapse clause, by contrast, is proposed for back-port (see Part 1 top note).
- **Evidence-extraction and confidentiality (§1.5):** the sponsoring delivery lead/account manager for the engagement — not the engineer alone — is responsible for expediting any redaction/export sign-off a badge's evidence needs (a decision record, a mentoring-relationship log, or facilitated-session notes held in a client's own tooling). If a client's contract terms make evidence extraction impossible for a required PF, use the in-place attestation path in §1.5 rather than treating the badge as unearnable.
- **Access-loss vs. confidentiality (§1.5a):** if an engineer rotates off a client and loses system access before evidence could be extracted or reviewed in place at all — with no confidentiality issue involved, just offboarding — this is an access-loss case, not a §1.5 confidentiality case. Route it through §1.7 (assignment-limited) instead of requiring an in-place review that's no longer physically possible.
- **Assignment-limited badges (§1.7):** a badge blocked by the engineer's current assignment — no available mentee, no client-facing facilitation opportunity, a mid-rotation client swap that cut a bundle/cycle short, or a required single occurrence (a strategy-referencing conversation, a client-imposed constraint) that simply didn't arise — for more than one review cycle is recorded as **"blocked — assignment-limited"**, or, for a single missing occurrence within an otherwise-clearable badge, as an **N/A** on that specific sub-item — distinct from both "not earned" and "failed." Applied explicitly on every PF 3 row from P3 up (see the note at the top of that table), on every PF 1/PF 2 row whose evidence is a bundle or cycle-continuity record, and now explicitly on LEAD-1-P2, LEAD-1-P5, and LEAD-2-P5 for their respective single-occurrence dependencies. The manager/theme owner tracks reassignment or an agreed alternate evidence substitute at the same review cadence as the badge/IDP itself.
- **Permanently-siloed roles (§1.6a):** for a P6/P7 candidate the manager/theme owner determines is genuinely permanently siloed on a single account (not mid-rotation), the §1.8 services-signal path is the expected route to the badge, not a fallback behind the internal cross-team/cross-account artifact — applied to LEAD-1-P6/P7, LEAD-2-P6/P7, and LEAD-3-P6/P7 above. Where this collapses the co-signer and the §1.8 evidence source into one person, the technical verifier's independent client-contact interview (see §1.6a) is required before sign-off.
- **Protected time:** time spent assembling badge evidence (documenting a decision record, collecting a mentee's confirmation, writing up a facilitated session) or preparing for a live/structured-conversation-checklist badge session is expected to be accounted for as part of normal professional development time — not unpaid overtime, and not something to negotiate separately with the client. The same protection extends to time spent identifying and packaging portfolio evidence for P5+ review. Schedule it by coordinating with the engineer's delivery/account lead.
- **Adoption claims (§1.6):** any badge whose completion bar or Certifies line depends on adoption/spread/culture language requires the 3-part adoption-evidence artifact (named contact, dated reference, what changed) — see the Part 2 rows above for where this applies. Where that claim is durative ("ensures," "fosters," ongoing-state language) rather than a demonstrated single instance, the underlying evidence must actually support an ongoing claim (multiple dated instances) or the Certifies line is worded as a demonstrated instance instead — see LEAD-1-P6 and LEAD-3-P6 for the applied fix.
- **No retroactive downgrade requirement:** badges are not re-litigated when the underlying standard is revised — a badge earned under a prior standard version remains valid unless the theme lead flags a material gap.
- **Relationship to promotion:** badges are evidence *inputs* to a promotion/GAP decision, not a substitute for it — a promotion decision still requires the full portfolio review in each PF's `03-portfolio-requirements.md`; the badges for that level are a fast way to confirm the underlying bars were already cleared. Per this repository's own IDP standard, badge records (and any evaluation-record scores) never get copied into a person's IDP — only narrative findings do.
- **Relationship to training:** completing training materials does not itself earn a badge — badges attach only to assessed, real work product per §1.3 above.
