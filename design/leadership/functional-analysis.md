# Functional Analysis: Leadership

> [Competency index](../index.md) › [Leadership](./index.md)

**Version date:** 2026-07-06

One-line purpose: an engineer who has this competency makes and owns decisions with awareness of bias, drives alignment to organizational strategy and goals, improves practices and processes, facilitates productive discussions, and mentors others — at a level appropriate to their career stage.

**Domain classification:** Core — occupation-defining for every engineering role at LFT; every row has a defined expectation from at least P4 up, and most from P2 up.

**Source:** `competencies/industry-evidence/leadership.md` (grounded 2026-07-06, all six levels as separate passes). Per that brief's scope note, this competency maps to the whole of Section 4 "Leadership" in `competencies/lft-engineering-competency-matrix.md` — five rows (Decision Making, Driving Alignment, Process Thinking, Facilitation, Mentoring) with **no named subgroups** in the source matrix, unlike Delivery, Strategic Impact, and Feedback/Communication/Collaboration, which all had matrix-provided groupings to start from.

**Assessability check (done during Phase A, from first principles — no matrix grouping to defer to):** with no subgroup structure given, Primary Function boundaries here are decided purely by which rows share an assessment mechanism. Decision Making, Driving Alignment, and Process Thinking are all best assessed through a structured judgment conversation cross-checked against a real artifact (a decision record, a strategy-linked planning conversation, a process-improvement proposal) — the same "conversation + artifact" mode used for Strategic Impact's Business Acumen & Strategy PF. Facilitation is fundamentally different: it's a skill you watch happen live (running an actual or simulated discussion), not one you discuss retrospectively — the same reasoning that kept Technical Skill's live-coding-adjacent PFs separate from its portfolio-only ones. Mentoring is different again: its evidence is relationship-outcome-based (a mentee's growth, a redundancy plan that worked) and shows up over a much longer time horizon than a single conversation or a single meeting — closer to a reference-check or portfolio review than either of the other two modes. Conclusion: three Primary Functions, grouped by assessment mechanism rather than by any matrix-given label: PF 1 (Judgment & Organizational Improvement — conversation + artifact), PF 2 (Facilitation — live observation), PF 3 (Mentoring — portfolio/outcome evidence).

**Phase A grammar calibration note (continuing the discipline established in Strategic Impact and Feedback/Communication/Collaboration):** sub-functions below split a matrix cell into multiple sub-functions only when the clauses describe genuinely different actions, artifacts, or moments. Two "See [level]" / combined-level patterns are preserved rather than force-expanded: Decision Making's and Driving Alignment's P3–P4 (and near-identical P2/P3) combined expectations, and Facilitation's explicit "P5: See P4."

## PF 1 — Judgment & Organizational Improvement

Make and own decisions with awareness of bias, drive alignment to organizational strategy and goals, and improve practices and processes — from explaining bias/accountability concepts at P2, up to owning organization-wide decisions, alignment, and process improvement at P7.

**Scope:** Decision Making; Driving Alignment; Process Thinking. Excludes the live-facilitation skill covered in PF 2 and the mentoring relationship covered in PF 3.

**Note (Phase A):** 26 sub-functions across 3 matrix rows — above the "≈4–10" guideline, an accepted exception for the same reason as Strategic Impact's single PF (25 subs) and FCC's Collaboration PF (28 subs): each row runs two or more parallel threads (e.g., Decision Making's bias-awareness thread and accountability-ownership thread) that each have genuine per-level deltas, and the assessability check above confirms all three rows are still reviewable together in one structured conversation despite the count.

Sub-functions:

*Decision Making — bias/objectivity thread:*
- 1.1 [P2] Explain how biases affect decision making, when asked
- 1.2 [P3] Reflect on their own biases when making a real decision, striving for objectivity — day-one expectation through P4
- 1.3 [P5] Raise awareness, within their team, of how biases impact decisions

*Decision Making — accountability/ownership thread:*
- 1.4 [P2] Explain what accountability for decisions and outcomes means, when asked
- 1.5 [P3] Hold themselves accountable for their own decisions and outcomes — day-one expectation through P4
- 1.6 [P5] Take ownership of decisions made in their team, helping teammates make clear decisions aligned with organizational goals
- 1.7 [P6] Take ownership of decisions made across teams
- 1.8 [P6] Ensure accountability is practiced throughout those teams
- 1.9 [P7] Take ownership of decisions made in the engineering organization
- 1.10 [P7] Ensure accountability is practiced throughout the organization

*Driving Alignment — strategy-conversation thread:*
- 1.11 [P2] Contribute to conversations based on organizational strategy and principles, when appropriate, staying strongly oriented towards goals
- 1.12 [P3] Initiate conversations based on organizational strategy and principles, when appropriate, staying strongly oriented towards goals
- 1.13 [P4] Have conversations based on organizational strategy and principles to ensure team alignment
- 1.14 [P5] Foster a culture within their team of having conversations based on organizational strategy and principles to create alignment
- 1.15 [P6] Foster this culture across several teams
- 1.16 [P7] Foster this culture across the organization

*Driving Alignment — ensuring continuous goal-work thread:*
- 1.17 [P4] Ensure their team is continuously working towards their shared goals
- 1.18 [P6] Ensure several teams are continuously working towards their goals
- 1.19 [P7] Ensure goals are understood and continuously worked towards across the organization

*Process Thinking:*
- 1.20 [P2] Explain their team's practices and processes, when asked
- 1.21 [P3] Discuss an improvement to team practices or processes with the team, sometimes
- 1.22 [P4] Discuss improvements to team practices and processes with the team, regularly
- 1.23 [P5] Collaborate with others, sometimes, to improve organizational practices and processes
- 1.24 [P6] Discuss improvements to practices or processes that affect several teams, with appropriate parties
- 1.25 [P6] Drive implementation of a cross-team practice or process improvement
- 1.26 [P7] Take ownership and responsibility for organizational practices and processes and their continuous improvement

## PF 2 — Facilitation

Facilitate discussions so that everyone is heard, outcomes tie to goals, and — at senior levels — cross-team and organization-wide discussions converge on decisions with buy-in.

**Scope:** Facilitation only. Explicitly not applicable at P2–P3 per the LFT matrix. Excludes the judgment/alignment/process content in PF 1 and the mentoring relationship in PF 3.

**Note (Phase A):** 4 sub-functions — the smallest PF in the pipeline so far, a direct consequence of the row being n/a at P2–P3 and restating "See P4" at P5 rather than a sign of under-decomposition. Kept as its own PF despite the small count because its assessment mechanism (watching a live facilitation) is categorically different from PF 1's conversation-based mode and PF 3's outcome-based mode.

Sub-functions:
- 2.1 [P4] Facilitate discussions within their team, ensuring everyone has an opportunity to share their opinion and be heard, that discussion outcomes tie to stated goals, and that no one person dominates the conversation — day-one expectation through P5 (matrix states "P5: See P4" verbatim)
- 2.2 [P6] Facilitate discussions across teams, ensuring relevant parties are included
- 2.3 [P6] Guide discussions toward decisions, clarifying points and securing buy-in
- 2.4 [P7] Facilitate organization-wide discussions, applying the same principles

## PF 3 — Mentoring

Seek out mentorship for one's own growth, and mentor others — widening in scope from teammates to the whole organization, and eventually fostering mentoring as an organizational culture and developing other mentors.

**Scope:** Mentoring only. Excludes the judgment/alignment/process content in PF 1 and the live-facilitation skill in PF 2.

**Note (Phase A):** 10 sub-functions across 1 matrix row — within reach of the "≈4–10" guideline (slightly over, given two parallel threads: personal mentoring practice, and deliberate mentoring-for-redundancy/culture).

Sub-functions:

*Personal mentoring practice (mentee → mentor, scope-widening):*
- 3.1 [P2] Seek out mentorship to grow their own experience
- 3.2 [P3] Mentor teammates, sometimes, in an open, respectful, flexible, and empathetic manner
- 3.3 [P4] Mentor teammates, reliably, in an open, respectful, flexible, and empathetic manner
- 3.4 [P5] Mentor teammates and members of other teams, as needed
- 3.5 [P6] Mentor across teams
- 3.6 [P7] Mentor across the organization

*Deliberate mentoring-for-redundancy and culture-fostering:*
- 3.7 [P4] Seek out mentoring opportunities specifically to create team redundancy and backfill ability — day-one expectation through P5
- 3.8 [P6] Foster a culture of mentoring across teams by seeking out mentoring opportunities for themselves and others
- 3.9 [P7] Foster an organizational culture of mentoring
- 3.10 [P7] Support others in their growth as mentors

## Related

- Prerequisite competencies: Feedback, Communication & Collaboration's PF 1 (Feedback) and PF 3 (Collaboration) are close companions to this competency's Mentoring and Facilitation PFs respectively — mentoring without good feedback delivery, or facilitation without healthy disagreement-handling, would be hollow. The competencies reinforce rather than gate each other.
- Downstream competencies: PF 1 (Judgment & Organizational Improvement), at P6/P7 scope, overlaps with Strategic Impact's Strategic Work row — both describe participating in and eventually leading organizational-level decisions. The distinction: Strategic Impact's Strategic Work is about engineering *strategy* specifically (what to build, technical direction), while this competency's Decision Making/Process Thinking is about organizational *judgment and operating practice* generally (how decisions get made, how work gets done) — related but not redundant.
- Cross-domain overlaps: Facilitation (PF 2) is a specific, observable subset of the broader communication skill in Feedback/Communication & Collaboration's PF 2 (Communication) — that PF covers communicating clearly in general; this PF covers the specific meeting-leadership skill of facilitating a group discussion toward a goal.
