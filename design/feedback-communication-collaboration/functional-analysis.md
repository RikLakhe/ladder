# Functional Analysis: Feedback, Communication & Collaboration

> [Competency index](../index.md) › [Feedback, Communication & Collaboration](./index.md)

**Version date:** 2026-07-06

One-line purpose: an engineer who has this competency gives and seeks feedback usefully, communicates and shares knowledge clearly across growing audiences, and collaborates — helping teammates, building relationships, and handling disagreement productively — at a level appropriate to their career stage.

**Domain classification:** Core — occupation-defining for every engineering role at LFT; every row in this section has a defined expectation at every level from P2 up (no row is purely a prerequisite for something else, and none is shared only incidentally with other roles).

**Source:** `competencies/industry-evidence/feedback-communication-collaboration.md` (grounded 2026-07-06, all six levels as separate passes). Per that brief's scope note, this competency maps to the whole of Section 3 "Feedback, Communication & Collaboration" in `competencies/lft-engineering-competency-matrix.md`, which contains three named groups — Feedback (Delivering Feedback; Seeking & Receiving Feedback), Communication (Effective Communication; Knowledge Sharing), and Collaboration (Teamwork; Relationship Building; Handling Disagreement) — seven rows total, the most of any competency grounded in this pipeline so far.

**Assessability check (done during Phase A, not deferred to Phase C):** applying the same test used for every competency in this pipeline: can each named group be watched or reviewed together in one exercise? Feedback (giving + seeking/receiving) — yes, a single feedback-exchange observation or 1:1 review naturally covers both giving and receiving. Communication (effective communication + knowledge sharing) — yes, a communication-sample review (a written doc, a presentation, a team conversation) naturally shows both how someone communicates and how they share knowledge. Collaboration (teamwork + relationship building + handling disagreement) — yes, a structured collaboration conversation or portfolio review ("how do you help teammates, what relationships have you built, how have you navigated disagreement") covers all three the same way Delivery's Self-Organization PF combined Reliability and Economic Thinking, and Strategic Impact kept three rows in one PF. Conclusion: keep LFT's own three-group structure as three Primary Functions, matching the matrix's own headings.

**Phase A grammar calibration note:** this competency's source rows contain more multi-sentence matrix cells than any prior competency (e.g., Teamwork and Handling Disagreement each pack 2–3 distinct behaviors into one matrix cell at several levels). Sub-functions below split a matrix cell into multiple sub-functions only when the clauses describe genuinely different actions, artifacts, or moments (e.g., "gives credit" vs. "provides on-call support" are different commitments happening at different times); clauses describing facets of the same single act in the same moment (e.g., "communicates clearly, listens actively, and attends to nonverbal cues" during one conversation) are kept as one sub-function with a compound description, consistent with how prior competencies handled compound object lists. Where the matrix restates an identical expectation across consecutive levels with no delta (e.g., on-call support at P3/P4/P5, or the non-defensive-disagreement stance at P3/P4), the sub-function is tagged once at its minimum level rather than duplicated.

## PF 1 — Feedback

Give feedback usefully to a widening audience, and seek/use feedback as a tool for growth — eventually fostering both as a team, cross-team, and organizational culture.

**Scope:** Delivering Feedback; Seeking & Receiving Feedback. Excludes the broader communication and collaboration behaviors covered in PF 2 and PF 3.

**Note (Phase A):** 10 sub-functions across 2 matrix rows — within the methodology's "≈4–10" granularity guideline, the first PF in this pipeline to land inside it without needing an accepted exception.

Sub-functions:

*Delivering Feedback:*
- 1.1 [P2] Explain how to deliver praise and constructive feedback in a useful manner, when asked
- 1.2 [P3] Deliver praise and constructive feedback to their team, teammates, and manager, in a useful manner
- 1.3 [P4] Deliver praise and constructive feedback to their team, teammates, manager, and team's business stakeholders, when opportunities arise
- 1.4 [P5] Foster a culture of delivering praise and constructive feedback within their team and the team's business stakeholders, demonstrating these behaviors themselves
- 1.5 [P6] Foster a culture of delivering praise and constructive feedback across several teams
- 1.6 [P7] Foster a culture of delivering praise and constructive feedback across the organization

*Seeking & Receiving Feedback (matrix gives one combined expectation for P2–P4; tagged at its minimum level, not force-split):*
- 1.7 [P2] Seek out feedback from teammates and manager, and apply it as a tool for growth — day-one expectation through P4
- 1.8 [P5] Foster, within their team and with its business stakeholders, a culture of seeking out feedback and using it for growth, demonstrating these behaviors themselves
- 1.9 [P6] Foster this culture across several teams and with their business stakeholders
- 1.10 [P7] Foster this culture across the organization

## PF 2 — Communication

Communicate clearly to a widening audience, share knowledge and contribute to documentation, and eventually foster both as a team, cross-team, and organizational culture.

**Scope:** Effective Communication; Knowledge Sharing. Excludes the feedback-specific and collaboration-specific behaviors covered in PF 1 and PF 3.

**Note (Phase A):** 15 sub-functions across 2 matrix rows — above the "≈4–10" guideline, an accepted exception for the same reason as prior competencies: P5–P7 in both rows split a personal-execution action from a distinct culture-fostering action once the matrix introduces "fosters a culture of..." language, which the assessability check above still treats as reviewable together in one portfolio pass.

Sub-functions:

*Effective Communication:*
- 2.1 [P2] Communicate clearly, concisely, and in an audience-oriented way in written and verbal form, actively listening to others and attending to nonverbal communication
- 2.2 [P3] Communicate clearly, concisely, and in an audience-oriented way in written and verbal form, usually, on both technical and non-technical subjects
- 2.3 [P4] Communicate clearly, concisely, and in an audience-oriented way in written and verbal form, consistently, on both technical and non-technical subjects
- 2.4 [P5] Communicate effectively with a diverse team
- 2.5 [P5] Foster a culture of clear, concise, audience-oriented communication on their team
- 2.6 [P6] Communicate effectively with a diverse set of teams
- 2.7 [P6] Foster a culture of effective communication across several teams
- 2.8 [P7] Communicate effectively across the company
- 2.9 [P7] Foster a culture of effective communication across the company

*Knowledge Sharing:*
- 2.10 [P2] Share knowledge of their area of work frequently with teammates
- 2.11 [P3] Contribute to their team's documentation, watching for opportunities to share knowledge beyond routine sharing
- 2.12 [P4] Encourage others to share knowledge and contribute to documentation
- 2.13 [P5] Foster a culture of documentation and knowledge sharing within their team and with the team's business stakeholders
- 2.14 [P6] Foster a culture of documentation and knowledge sharing across several teams
- 2.15 [P7] Foster a culture of documentation and knowledge sharing across the organization

## PF 3 — Collaboration

Help teammates, build and leverage relationships across a widening scope, and handle disagreement productively — eventually fostering all three as a team, cross-team, and organizational culture.

**Scope:** Teamwork; Relationship Building; Handling Disagreement. Excludes the feedback-specific and communication-specific behaviors covered in PF 1 and PF 2.

**Note (Phase A):** 28 sub-functions across 3 matrix rows — the largest single-PF count in the pipeline so far (previously Strategic Impact's 25). Accepted for the same reasons as prior exceptions, plus one specific to this PF: Teamwork and Handling Disagreement each bundle 2–3 genuinely distinct behaviors per matrix cell at several levels (e.g., "helps with tasks," "gives credit," and "provides on-call support" are three different commitments, not three facets of one act) — splitting these was a direct, deliberate response to the compound-behavior finding caught in Strategic Impact's Phase C Pass 1, applied proactively here rather than caught after the fact.

Sub-functions:

*Teamwork:*
- 3.1 [P2] Help teammates when requested
- 3.2 [P2] Give or share credit where due
- 3.3 [P3] Help teammates overcome obstacles, resolve blockers, and complete work tasks, when requested
- 3.4 [P3] Provide on-call support as determined by Engineering Management — day-one expectation through P5
- 3.5 [P4] Help teammates overcome obstacles, resolve blockers, and complete work tasks, sometimes
- 3.6 [P5] Help teammates overcome obstacles, resolve blockers, and complete work tasks, consistently
- 3.7 [P6] Work across teams, consistently, to help them resolve blockers and complete work tasks
- 3.8 [P6] Ensure credit is shared where due, across teams
- 3.9 [P7] Work across the organization, consistently, to enable teams to support each other

*Relationship Building:*
- 3.10 [P2] Build strong relationships with teammates and manager
- 3.11 [P3] Build strong relationships with teammates, manager, and their product counterpart
- 3.12 [P4] Build strong relationships with teammates, manager, and their team's relevant business stakeholders
- 3.13 [P5] Build and improve strong relationships with teammates, manager, business stakeholders, and senior engineers across the organization
- 3.14 [P5] Leverage relationships to better plan for and position their team
- 3.15 [P6] Build and improve strong relationships with engineers and managers across the organization
- 3.16 [P6] Leverage relationships to better plan for and position those teams
- 3.17 [P7] Build and improve strong relationships across the organization
- 3.18 [P7] Leverage relationships to better plan for and position the engineering organization

*Handling Disagreement:*
- 3.19 [P2] Share opinions openly and contribute to discussions in a respectful manner
- 3.20 [P2] Work with teammates to resolve disagreement in a healthy manner
- 3.21 [P2] Remain open to changing their perspective based on others' input
- 3.22 [P3] Approach disagreement with teammates non-defensively and with inquisitiveness, using contradictory opinions as a basis for constructive conversation — day-one expectation through P4
- 3.23 [P4] Encourage teammates to openly share their opinions
- 3.24 [P5] Foster a culture within their team where people share opinions openly, approach disagreement non-defensively, and use contradictory opinions constructively
- 3.25 [P6] Foster this culture across several teams
- 3.26 [P6] Work through surface-level disagreements to expose the concerns of disagreeing voices
- 3.27 [P7] Foster this culture across the organization
- 3.28 [P7] Integrate disagreeing perspectives from across the company into their own perspective and plans

## Related

- Prerequisite competencies: none formally required. Delivery's Self-Organization PF (proactive communication of blockers) overlaps in spirit with this competency's Delivering Feedback and Effective Communication rows, but the two reinforce rather than gate each other.
- Downstream competencies: PF 3's Relationship Building and Handling Disagreement rows, at P6/P7 scope, are natural inputs to any future "Organizational Leadership" competency where managing organization-wide relationships and conflict becomes the primary job.
- Cross-domain overlaps: Strategic Impact's Business Acumen & Strategy PF assumes a baseline of effective communication (PF 2 here) to translate technical tradeoffs into business terms — PF 2 is the more foundational, general-purpose skill; Strategic Impact's version is scoped specifically to business/strategy content.
