# Feedback, Communication & Collaboration — Autonomous Projects

4 open-ended projects. No step-by-step guide — acceptance criteria only. Project 1 spans all three PFs (the whole-ladder P2–P4 bundle discipline); Projects 2, 3, and 4 are P5-stretch, one per culture-fostering PF. Each project is written so that, once complete, the project's artifact IS (or directly feeds) the evidence bundle/portfolio item a specific badge requires — see the "Badges this feeds" line on each and `05-badge-reference-card.md` for the full map.

Completing a project does not itself award a badge — the named instrument's Verifier still has to review the resulting artifact and sign off, per `../assessment/badges.md` §1.3, including the co-signer requirement at Tier 3 (P4) where it applies.

## Project 1 — Run one real 4–6 week bundle across all three PFs simultaneously, end to end

**[P2 target] with buddy/manager input on what counts / [P3 target] largely self-directed / [P4 target] unaided, including the outside-immediate-team and multi-engagement items**

**Maps to:** PF 1 (Feedback), PF 2 (Communication), PF 3 (Collaboration) — the whole competency, not one PF
**Badges this feeds:** FCC-1-P2/P3/P4, FCC-2-P2/P3/P4, FCC-3-P2/P3/P4 depending on how deep the bundle goes — this project's acceptance criteria ARE the `00-evidence-bundle-standard.md` requirement, not a separate exercise leading up to it

**Brief:** Pick one real 4–6 week working window and, throughout it, build a single coherent dated log spanning feedback, communication, and collaboration — not three disconnected logs assembled after the fact. This is the real-work version of Guided Exercises 1–5, run as one sustained habit rather than five separate sessions.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- The bundle spans a real, continuous 4–6 week window, chosen and committed to before it started, not selected retroactively from a longer period
- Feedback: ≥2 dated, SBI-shaped feedback-given instances + 1 feedback-sought instance with a described change; at P4, ≥1 instance extends to a stakeholder outside the immediate team, adapted for that audience
- Communication: ≥1 audience-aware sample, ≥1 knowledge-sharing/documentation instance, and a dated async cross-timezone handoff note (required regardless of level or client contact); at P3+, ≥1 calibrated client-facing artifact if the engagement has client contact; at P4, ≥2 dated client-ready communications produced without a rewrite from the candidate's lead, plus a bad-news/escalation instance
- Collaboration: dated instances of helping, credit-sharing, a level-appropriate relationship, and both a disagreement-resolved and a separate mind-changed instance; at P3, on-call record or documented non-rostered status, and a client-structure-adaptation instance if client-facing; at P4, a proactive-help instance and — if multi-staffed — a multi-engagement boundary-keeping instance
- At least one instance across the bundle involves someone outside the candidate's closest working pair (a different teammate, a client stakeholder, a cross-timezone counterpart), per `00-evidence-bundle-standard.md` §2
- Every instance has a one-line dated log entry with a real artifact link/reference, assembled as it happened — not reconstructed the night before review

**What a good solution looks like:** see all three PFs' `01-work-product-review-rubric.md` files (this project's log IS the bundle those rubrics review) and the corresponding `02-live-demo-checklist.md`/structured-conversation files, which cross-check the bundle rather than scoring a standalone performance.

## Project 2 — Introduce a team communication or documentation norm and get someone else to adopt it

**[P5 target — stretch assignment, real scope required]**

**Maps to:** PF 2 — Communication (effective communication and knowledge-sharing culture rows)
**Badges this feeds:** FCC-2-P5 (Team Communication & Documentation Culture Architect)

**Brief:** Introduce a real, durable communication or documentation norm on your team — an escalation protocol, a handoff template, a documentation standard, a client-reporting cadence — and get at least one teammate to adopt it without you prompting each instance. Self-modeling alone, no matter how consistent, does not satisfy this project.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- The norm is documented somewhere durable (a written standard, a template, a linked doc) — not just something you personally do consistently
- At least one teammate visibly follows the norm on their own initiative, in a real instance you can point to and date — not you reminding them each time
- You can describe what existed before the norm and what changed, and how you know it changed (not just that you introduced it)
- If the norm touches client-facing communication or cross-timezone handoffs, at least one instance shows it holding up in a real client-facing or distributed-team context, not only internally
- You can describe at least one piece of pushback or friction you got introducing the norm, and how you handled it

**What a good solution looks like:** see `../assessment/pf2-communication/03-portfolio-requirements.md` (rows for 2.5, 2.13, and the client-facing/cross-timezone-design items) and the rubric note that self-modeling alone does not clear any culture-fostering row. This project is explicitly a P5 stretch assignment — "good" here means real, reviewable adoption by someone else, not a polished writeup of your own habit.

## Project 3 — Coach a named teammate on client-team structure or multi-engagement collaboration

**[P5 target — stretch assignment, real scope required]**

**Maps to:** PF 3 — Collaboration (the new P5 coaching-on-client-structure row, plus relationship-building and disagreement-culture rows)
**Badges this feeds:** FCC-3-P5 (Team Collaboration Culture Builder)

**Brief:** Identify a real teammate who is newly navigating a client's team structure (adapting to a client's ceremonies/tools/conventions) or newly staffed on multiple concurrent engagements, and coach them through it — a real, documented conversation or paired session, not a restatement of your own adaptation experience with their name attached to it. The portfolio requirements are explicit that this row is not satisfied by describing your own client-structure adaptation.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- A named teammate is identified, and the coaching interaction (a specific conversation, a paired session, a written note) is documented with a date
- The teammate — or their manager — can corroborate that the coaching happened and describe what it covered
- At least one instance shows the coached teammate visibly changing their own practice afterward (adapting to the client's structure differently, or handling a multi-engagement boundary differently) as a result of the coaching — not just having received advice
- Separately, evidence exists of the candidate consistently helping teammates (peer feedback specifically noting reliability, not just occasional helpfulness) and building/leveraging at least one relationship with a senior engineer or stakeholder outside the immediate team for the team's benefit
- If the team has a live disagreement-handling norm the candidate fostered, at least one instance shows a teammate adopting non-defensive disagreement-handling unprompted

**What a good solution looks like:** see `../assessment/pf3-collaboration/03-portfolio-requirements.md`, specifically the "Evidence of coaching teammates on client-team structure and multi-engagement collaboration" row and its explicit note: "a description of the candidate's own client-structure adaptation, without a named teammate on the receiving end, does not satisfy this row." This project is explicitly a P5 stretch assignment — plan for it to take several weeks of real relationship-building before the coaching moment even happens naturally.

## Project 4 — Foster a feedback-giving/feedback-seeking culture on your team, and coach a teammate on matrixed-feedback reconciliation

**[P5 target — stretch assignment, real scope required]**

**Maps to:** PF 1 — Feedback (the P5 feedback-giving-culture, feedback-seeking-culture, and matrixed-feedback-coaching rows)
**Badges this feeds:** FCC-1-P5 (Team Feedback Culture Builder)

**Brief:** This is PF1's own P5 training unit — it is not borrowed from Project 2's PF2 norm-adoption work, because Project 2's acceptance criteria are about communication/documentation norms and don't map to PF1 P5's actual portfolio rows. Introduce or strengthen a real feedback-giving or feedback-seeking practice on your team (a retro format, a peer-review norm, a habit of naming what changed after feedback) and get at least one teammate to visibly adopt it unprompted. Separately, identify a real teammate navigating a matrixed reporting structure (client-directed day-to-day work, LFT-owned formal record) and coach them on how to separate client-side feedback from the formal LFT record and get it surfaced to their LFT manager — a real, documented interaction, not a restatement of your own matrixed-reconciliation habit with their name attached.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- Feedback-giving or feedback-seeking culture: the practice/norm is documented somewhere durable (a retro template, a written feedback guide, a stated team norm) and at least one teammate visibly adopts it unprompted, in a real dated instance you can point to — self-modeling alone does not satisfy this
- You can describe what existed before the norm and what changed, and how you know it changed (not just that you introduced it)
- Matrixed-feedback coaching: a named teammate in a matrixed structure is identified, and the coaching interaction (a specific conversation, a paired session, a written note) is documented with a date, distinct from the candidate's own matrixed-reconciliation practice restated with their name attached
- The coached teammate — or their manager — can corroborate that the coaching happened and describe what it covered, and at least one instance shows the teammate separating client-side feedback from the formal record, or surfacing it to their LFT manager, differently afterward as a result of the coaching
- If no teammate on a matrixed engagement is available this window, that constraint is stated explicitly (a valid, non-failing state per `../assessment/badges.md` §1.7) rather than a fabricated coaching instance — but the feedback-giving/seeking-culture criterion above still must be met in full; this project isn't complete on the culture item alone
- You can describe at least one piece of pushback or friction you got introducing the norm, and how you handled it

**What a good solution looks like:** see `../assessment/pf1-feedback/03-portfolio-requirements.md`, specifically the "Evidence of fostering a feedback-giving culture on their team," "Evidence of fostering a feedback-seeking culture on their team," and "Coaching teammates on matrixed feedback" rows, and the rubric note that self-modeling alone does not clear any culture-fostering row. This project is explicitly a P5 stretch assignment — plan for the culture-adoption evidence and the matrixed-feedback coaching moment to both take real weeks to occur naturally, not a single sitting.
