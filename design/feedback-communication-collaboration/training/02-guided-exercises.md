# Feedback, Communication & Collaboration — Guided Exercises

5 exercises, ordered simplest to most complex. Each maps to at least one PF sub-function from `../functional-analysis.md` **and** to the badge(s) it builds evidence toward (see `05-badge-reference-card.md` for the full map). Uses the team's real feedback channels, documentation platform, and status/async communication tooling per `../../COMPANY-CONTEXT.md`.

**None of these exercises award a badge by themselves.** Completing one produces (or extends) the artifact a badge's instrument requires. The badge still needs the named Verifier's sign-off per `../assessment/badges.md` §1.3 — and at every level covered here, that sign-off is against a **dated 4–6 week evidence bundle** (`../assessment/00-evidence-bundle-standard.md`), not a single exercise session. Exercises 1–5 below are designed to accumulate into that bundle as you go, not to be thrown away after the exercise ends.

**Before starting any exercise:** pick your 4–6 week window now, and start a one-line dated log (date, what happened, who was involved, artifact link) the moment you begin. Every exercise below assumes this log already exists and is being added to in real time.

## Exercise 1 — Start the feedback bundle: give and log ≥2 dated instances (PF 1)

**Maps to sub-functions:** 1.1 [P2], 1.2 [P3], 1.7 [P2–P4]
**Builds toward badges:** FCC-1-P2 (Feedback Literacy) directly; extended to ≥2 instances, feeds FCC-1-P3 (Feedback Giver)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `../assessment/pf1-feedback/01-work-product-review-rubric.md` — "Giving feedback usefully" and "Seeking and using feedback" rows, evaluated across the whole bundle, not a best-case anecdote

**Goal:** Build the core of your feedback bundle: ≥2 dated, specific, SBI-shaped feedback instances given to a teammate or your LFT lead/manager, plus 1 dated instance of you seeking feedback and describing what you changed.

**Setup:** Confirm your 4–6 week window (same window as Exercises 2–5, if you're building all three PFs' bundles together per Autonomous Project 1). Have concept notes §1–2 fresh.

**Step-by-step:**
1. The next time you notice a specific, real moment worth giving feedback on (a PR, a demo, a handoff, a decision someone made), give it within a day or two of noticing, not weeks later. Use the SBI shape (§1): situation, behavior, impact, and — if constructive — a suggested change.
2. Log it immediately: date, who, what you said, link to the artifact (PR comment, 1:1 note, written message) if one exists.
3. Repeat for a second, distinct instance later in the window — different person or different context than instance 1, so the bundle shows range, not one relationship repeated.
4. Separately, pick a real piece of your own work and ask someone specific for feedback on it (not a generic "any feedback?"). After you get it, write one or two sentences on what you actually changed as a result — this is the part the rubric checks, not the asking itself.
5. Before the review session, re-read your two feedback-given log entries against the "Does not meet P2" column — would a reviewer read either as vague praise/criticism? If so, redo it for real before the review, don't just relabel it.

**Expected output:** ≥2 dated, documented feedback-given instances + 1 dated feedback-sought instance with a described change, all logged with real dates as they happened.

**Check:** could a reviewer, reading only your log and linked artifacts, tell exactly what happened and when — without you narrating it live? That's exactly what the rubric's pass bar checks.

## Exercise 2 — Build the collaboration bundle: helping, credit, relationship, and disagreement instances (PF 3)

**Maps to sub-functions:** 3.1, 3.2, 3.10, 3.19, 3.20, 3.21 [P2]; 3.3, 3.4, 3.11, 3.22 [P3]
**Builds toward badges:** FCC-3-P2 (Team Player Foundations) directly; extended per step 6, feeds FCC-3-P3 (Client-Embedded Collaborator)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `../assessment/pf3-collaboration/01-work-product-review-rubric.md` — helping, credit-sharing, relationship-building, and handling-disagreement rows

**Goal:** Build five to six distinct dated bundle instances: helping when asked, sharing credit, a relationship example, a disagreement worked through with a teammate, a separate instance of changing your mind based on someone's input, and (P3) on-call status or client-structure adaptation.

**Setup:** Same window as Exercise 1. Have concept notes §7–8 fresh.

**Step-by-step:**
1. The next time a teammate (LFT or client-side) asks for help with a blocker or task, help, and log it the same day: what they asked, what you did, outcome.
2. The next time credit for shared work comes up (a demo, a retro, a status update), explicitly name who contributed what, rather than letting attribution default to whoever's presenting. Log the instance.
3. Pick one relationship at the scope appropriate to your target level (teammate/manager at P2, add product counterpart at P3) and log one concrete, dated interaction that built or deepened it — not "we get along well," but a specific thing that happened.
4. Watch for a real disagreement during the window — in a PR review, a design discussion, a retro. When it happens, note (i) whether you shared your own opinion respectfully, and (ii) how the disagreement was worked through. Log it as it happens, not reconstructed afterward.
5. Separately, log a distinct instance where someone else's input changed your mind about something real — this must be a different event from item 4's disagreement-resolution instance, even if it happened in the same conversation.
6. **For P3:** if you're on-call, keep your on-call record for the window; if not yet rostered, note that explicitly (a valid, non-failing state per `../assessment/badges.md` §1.7). If staffed on a client engagement, log one instance of adapting to the client's existing tools/ceremonies rather than defaulting to LFT's own.

**Expected output:** 5–6 dated log entries covering help, credit, relationship, disagreement-resolved, mind-changed, and (P3) on-call status or client-structure adaptation.

**Check:** are the disagreement-resolved and mind-changed instances genuinely two different events, not the same anecdote split in two? Reviewers specifically check for this.

## Exercise 3 — Build the communication bundle: audience-aware samples, a documentation contribution, and a cross-timezone async handoff (PF 2)

**Maps to sub-functions:** 2.1 [P2], 2.10 [P2], 2.2 [P3], 2.11 [P3]
**Builds toward badges:** FCC-2-P2 (Audience-Aware Communicator) directly; extended per step 4, feeds FCC-2-P3 (Client & Cross-Timezone Communicator); extended further per step 5, feeds FCC-2-P4 (Consistent Cross-Audience Communicator)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `../assessment/pf2-communication/01-work-product-review-rubric.md` — audience-oriented communication, knowledge-sharing, and client-facing/cross-timezone rows; `02-live-demo-checklist.md`'s P4 bad-news/escalation prompt

**Goal:** Build a communication bundle with a clear written/verbal sample, a knowledge-sharing instance, a documentation contribution, a real async cross-timezone handoff note (non-negotiable at P2), and — extending to P4 — a second dated client-ready communication plus a real bad-news/escalation instance handled without a hand-holding rewrite from your lead.

**Setup:** Same window as Exercises 1–2. Have concept notes §4–6 fresh. Identify, in advance, one real moment where you'll need to hand off open work to someone in a different time zone (a teammate in a different region, or a US-based client contact) without a same-day reply available.

**Step-by-step:**
1. Pick one written or verbal communication sample from real work (a design write-up, a Slack explanation, a demo) where you can point to how you adapted it for the audience — log the artifact link and a one-line note on the adaptation choice.
2. The next time you learn something non-obvious that a teammate would benefit from, share it — and if it's durable enough to be worth finding again later, put it somewhere documented (wiki, README, runbook), not just a Slack message. Log both the sharing instance and, separately, the documentation contribution.
3. When your end-of-day/handoff moment arrives (per Setup), write the async handoff note: what shipped, what's open, what decision (if any) the receiving person needs to make, and enough context that they don't need to wait for a live call. Log it with the timezone gap noted (e.g., "Nepal → US, 11hr gap, no overlap that day").
4. **For P3:** if any of your work touches a client stakeholder, add one client-facing artifact (status update, ticket comment, email) and check it against concept notes §6a before sending — strip internal shorthand, no blame language, calibrated tone. If your engagement has no client contact, this item is N/A per `../assessment/badges.md` §1.7 — don't manufacture one.
5. **For P4:** extend the client-facing artifact from step 4 to a second, distinct dated client-ready communication (a status report, a steering-committee-bound summary, or an escalation note) sent without a rewrite pass from your lead — log it separately, don't count the same artifact twice. Then, the next time you have a real piece of bad news to communicate (a slip, a defect, a missed expectation — internal or client-facing), log the instance: what you escalated internally first and to whom, then what you actually said to the affected audience, including the ask/next-step you framed it around (not only an apology or only technical justification). If no genuine bad-news moment arises during the window, note that explicitly and be ready to walk through a real past instance instead — don't fabricate one.

**Expected output:** 1 audience-aware sample, 1 knowledge-sharing instance, 1 documentation contribution, 1 dated async cross-timezone handoff note (required regardless of client contact), (P3, if applicable) 1 calibrated client-facing artifact, and (P4) a second dated client-ready communication plus 1 dated bad-news/escalation instance showing the internal-escalation-first sequence.

**Check:** could the person who received your async handoff note actually act on it without a same-day reply from you? If unsure, ask them after the fact whether they needed to follow up to understand it — that follow-up need is the tell that the note wasn't actually self-sufficient. For the P4 extension specifically: does your escalation log entry show who you told internally, and in what order, before the client/audience-facing message went out — not just the outward-facing message itself?

## Exercise 4 — Multi-engagement boundary-keeping instance (or proactive-help/stakeholder-relationship instance if single-staffed) (PF 3)

**Maps to sub-functions:** 3.5 [P4], 3.12 [P4], 3.23 [P4]
**Builds toward badge:** FCC-3-P4 (Proactive Cross-Boundary Collaborator)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `../assessment/pf3-collaboration/01-work-product-review-rubric.md` "Meets P4" rows, including "Multi-engagement boundary-keeping"

**Goal:** Log a proactive-help instance, a business-stakeholder relationship instance, an opinion-encouragement instance, and — if staffed on ≥2 concurrent client engagements — a dated multi-engagement boundary-keeping instance.

**Setup:** Same window as prior exercises. Have concept notes §9 and §9a fresh. Confirm your current staffing: single-engagement or multi-engagement — this changes which variant of step 4 applies.

**Step-by-step:**
1. Watch for a moment where you notice a teammate needs help before they ask for it — help proactively, and log it: what you noticed, what you did, and confirm afterward it wasn't unwelcome (proactive help that overrides someone's own plan isn't the goal).
2. Log a dated relationship-building instance with a business stakeholder relevant to your team (often a client-side stakeholder for engagement-based work) — a specific interaction, not a general description of the relationship. **This step is the P4-scope business-stakeholder relationship Exercise 5 depends on** — do it for real here before moving on, don't defer it.
3. In a real discussion where a quieter teammate hasn't spoken up, invite their opinion directly ("[name], what's your take on this?") and log whether/how it changed the discussion.
4. **If multi-staffed (≥2 concurrent client engagements):** the next time you catch yourself about to reference one engagement's context while working on another, stop, and log the moment you caught and avoided the leak — this is the specific, dated evidence the rubric wants, not a general claim of discretion. **If single-staffed:** mark this item N/A per `../assessment/badges.md` §1.7; do not manufacture a multi-engagement scenario.
5. Log an instance of embedded-client collaboration (working alongside a client-side engineer/PM as a peer, not just adapting to their tools per Exercise 2's P3 instance — this is about the collaborative act itself).

**Expected output:** 4–5 dated log entries: proactive help, stakeholder relationship, opinion-encouragement, embedded-client-collaboration, and (if applicable) multi-engagement boundary-keeping — or a clean N/A for the last item if single-staffed.

**Check:** for the multi-engagement item specifically — does your log entry describe a real, caught-in-the-moment near-miss or careful separation, not a generic assurance that you're "always careful about confidentiality"? Reviewers are specifically watching for the difference.

## Exercise 5 — Extend the feedback bundle to a stakeholder outside the immediate team, and reconcile matrixed feedback (PF 1)

**Maps to sub-functions:** 1.3 [P4]
**Builds toward badge:** FCC-1-P4 (Cross-Audience & Matrixed Feedback Navigator)
**Instrument this rehearses:** `../assessment/pf1-feedback/01-work-product-review-rubric.md` "Meets P4" row + `02-live-demo-checklist.md` P4 matrixed-context prompt

**Goal:** Extend the same feedback bundle from Exercise 1 with a dated instance of feedback given to someone outside your immediate team — often a client lead or PM — adapted for that audience, and, if you're in a matrixed reporting structure, a dated instance of relaying client-side feedback to your LFT manager.

**Setup:** Use the same bundle/window as Exercise 1, extended if needed. Have concept notes §3 and §10 fresh. Requires having a real business-stakeholder relationship already in place (per Exercise 4's step 2) — don't force this before you have one.

**Step-by-step:**
1. The next time you have real feedback for a business stakeholder (client lead, client PM, or an internal stakeholder outside engineering), deliver it, consciously adapting the framing for their context (concept notes §3) — less technical detail, more impact framing, careful tone.
2. Log it immediately: what you adapted and why, not just "gave feedback to the client."
3. If your reporting structure is matrixed (client directs day-to-day, LFT manager owns the formal record): the next time your client-side counterpart gives you feedback — praise or constructive — relay it to your LFT manager within the same week, in writing (a short Slack message or 1:1 note is enough). Log this as a separate dated instance.
4. If you have no matrixed structure, mark this item N/A per `../assessment/badges.md` §1.7 rather than fabricating one.
5. Before the structured conversation, rehearse both instances out loud once — the P4 prompts specifically ask "how did you adjust it for them" and "how do you make sure both [feedback sources] are accounted for."

**Expected output:** 1 dated instance of feedback given to someone outside the immediate team, with a described adaptation; 1 dated instance of relaying client-side feedback to the LFT manager (or a clean N/A if not matrixed).

**Check:** does your log entry name the specific adaptation you made (not just "I was careful"), and — if matrixed — can your LFT manager independently confirm they received the relayed feedback from you, not from someone else or not at all?
