# PF 2 — Communication: Live Demo Checklist

**Redesign note (2026-08-03):** this session now closes with a cross-check against the candidate's dated evidence bundle (`../00-evidence-bundle-standard.md`, assembled per `01-work-product-review-rubric.md`) so the live performance is corroborated by real, dated artifacts, not scored in isolation. A pass here plus the rubric bar is the evidence for badge `FCC-2-P2`/`P3`/`P4` in `../badges.md`.

**Used when:** technical interview or promotion review (30–40 min session). Unlike PF 1 and PF 3 in this competency, Communication lends itself to an actual live demonstration rather than only a retrospective conversation — explaining something is an act you can watch happen in real time.
**Covers:** P2–P4. (P5–P7 are assessed via portfolio + an observed team/cross-team communication — see `03-portfolio-requirements.md`.)

## Scenario: Explain a technical concept to a mixed audience

**Setup:** Ask the candidate to explain a piece of their own recent technical work twice: once to the interviewer as a technical peer, and once as if the interviewer were a non-technical stakeholder (product manager, support lead) — pitched, from P3 up, as an external client stakeholder rather than an internal one, to test tone calibration for an enterprise client audience. 30 minutes.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Technical explanation is clear, concise, and appropriately detailed for a peer audience
- [ ] Non-technical explanation drops jargon and reframes around impact/outcome rather than mechanism
- [ ] Actively checks whether the "audience" understood, rather than just delivering a monologue
- [ ] **P3+:** when the stakeholder is played as a client (not an internal PM), tone stays calibrated — no over-familiarity, no internal shorthand ("our sprint," tool/ticket names) left unexplained, no blaming another team or vendor in front of the client
- [ ] **P4 only:** adjusts fluidly when the interviewer (playing a confused stakeholder) asks a clarifying or skeptical question, without becoming defensive or over-technical
- [ ] **P4 only:** given a scenario where the explanation surfaces bad news (a slip, a defect, a missed expectation), candidate frames it with a clear ask/next step rather than only an apology or only technical justification, and can describe when they'd escalate internally before communicating it to the client
- [ ] **Closing cross-check:** at least one claim made during the live explanation (e.g. "I usually check for understanding this way," "I'd write this up async") is matched against a real, dated instance in the candidate's evidence bundle, not left as an unverified assertion

**Prompts:**
- "What part of that was hardest to translate for a non-technical audience, and why?"
- "Imagine this stakeholder is at the client, not internal, and you won't have a live call with them again for several hours because of the timezone gap — what would you put in a written update instead, and what would you leave for a call?"
- P4: "If this stakeholder pushed back and said it still didn't make sense, what would you try next?"
- P4: "If this update involved bad news for the client, walk me through how you'd escalate it internally first, and how that changes what you say to the client."
- "Show me the bundle instance where you actually did this on a real project."

**For the candidate:**
- Come with a piece of your own recent technical work you can explain — you'll be asked to explain it twice: once to the interviewer as a technical peer, once as if the interviewer were a non-technical stakeholder (PM, support lead); from P3 up, expect this to be framed as an external client stakeholder rather than an internal one
- Bring your dated evidence bundle (per `01-work-product-review-rubric.md`) — you'll be asked to point to a real instance backing at least one claim you make live
- Be ready to describe how you'd hand this off in writing if a live call wasn't available for 10+ hours (Nepal/India–US gap) — what goes in the async update versus what waits for a sync
- At P4, be ready to keep adapting fluidly if the "stakeholder" pushes back with a confused or skeptical question, without becoming defensive or over-technical, and be ready to talk through escalating a piece of bad news internally before it reaches the client
- Time budget: 30 minutes total (5 min setup, 20 min explanation + Q&A, 5 min wrap-up) — bring your evidence bundle, no other advance prep material required beyond picking the piece of work to explain

**Scoring:** pass/follow-up/fail per item. **Time budget:** 5 min setup, 20 min explanation + Q&A, 5 min wrap-up.

**Note on realism:** this scenario is deliberately run as a live synchronous conversation for assessment convenience; it does not substitute for async/cross-timezone evidence. Async handoff and client-facing writing quality are assessed via real artifacts in `01-work-product-review-rubric.md` and, at P5+, `03-portfolio-requirements.md` — do not treat a strong live performance here as sufficient evidence of async competence on its own, which is why the closing cross-check item above is mandatory, not optional.
