# PF 2 — Communication: Portfolio Requirements

**Redesign note (2026-08-03):** each row below is also the named evidence source for a P5/P6/P7 badge in `../badges.md` (`FCC-2-P5`, `FCC-2-P6`, `FCC-2-P7`). A badge is not awarded from this table alone — the reviewer sign-off and, for culture/adoption rows, the adoption-evidence artifact (`../badges.md` §1.6) are also required.

**Used when:** promotion review to P5/P6/P7, or lead/principal/architect-level hiring.

## Required evidence items

| Item | Maps to PC | Quality bar: sufficient | Quality bar: good |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence of communicating effectively with a diverse team | 2.4 [P5] | One example of communication adapted for a diverse audience | Recurring pattern, with explicit adaptation choices explainable after the fact |
| Evidence of client-facing communication and expectation management (P5+; "diverse team/teams" in 2.4/2.6 is read to include the client relationship, not only internal diversity) | 2.4/2.6 [P5–P6] | One example of a client-facing artifact (status update, escalation email, steering-committee readout) showing tone calibrated to an external enterprise audience, plus a description of when the candidate escalated internally versus communicated directly to the client | Recurring pattern across a client engagement, including at least one instance of managing client expectations proactively (surfacing risk before the client asks) and a described escalation protocol the candidate follows or has helped define |
| Evidence of cross-timezone/async communication design (P5+) | 2.4/2.6 [P5–P6] | One example of an async handoff or documentation practice designed for a 10+ hour timezone gap (e.g., Nepal/India to US), showing the candidate does not assume synchronous availability | A recurring async-first practice (handoff template, written EOD summary, recorded walkthrough) adopted by others on the team without the candidate driving each instance |
| Evidence of fostering a communication culture on their team | 2.5 [P5] | One example of a communication norm introduced, with at least one instance of a teammate following it without the candidate prompting them — not just the candidate's own introduction of the norm (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | A recurring norm the team now follows consistently without the candidate enforcing it each time |
| Evidence of fostering a documentation/knowledge-sharing culture on their team | 2.13 [P5] | One documentation norm or template introduced, with at least one instance of a teammate using or contributing to it unprompted — not just the candidate's own introduction (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | Team documentation quality/coverage measurably improved, attributable to the candidate's effort, and sustained by the team without the candidate driving each contribution |
| Evidence of communicating effectively with a diverse set of teams | 2.6 [P6] | One cross-team communication example | Recurring pattern across several teams |
| Evidence of fostering a communication culture across several teams | 2.7 [P6] | One cross-team norm or forum introduced, with at least one instance of someone on another team using it without the candidate's direct involvement — not just the candidate's own introduction (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | Adopted by multiple teams independently of the candidate, with a before/after description of what changed |
| Evidence of fostering a documentation culture across several teams | 2.14 [P6] | One cross-team documentation initiative, with at least one instance of someone on another team contributing to or maintaining it without the candidate's direct involvement (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | Adopted across several teams with measurable improvement, sustained without the candidate's ongoing involvement |
| (P7) Evidence of communicating effectively across the company | 2.8 [P7] | One company-wide communication example | Cited elsewhere in the company as clear and effective |
| (P6/P7) Evidence of communicating with and managing expectations across the client relationship at scale | 2.6/2.8 [P6–P7] | One example of shaping how a client engagement communicates — an escalation protocol, a steering-committee cadence, or a client-reporting standard the candidate defined or reset | Adopted as the standard approach across the account/engagement (or across several client engagements at P7), with a before/after description of how client trust or expectation-alignment changed |
| (P7) Evidence of fostering a communication culture across the company | 2.9 [P7] | One company-level initiative, with evidence that people outside the candidate's direct circle have adopted it unprompted (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | A lasting channel, forum, or norm now used company-wide, independent of the candidate's ongoing involvement |
| (P7) Evidence of fostering a documentation culture across the organization | 2.15 [P7] | One org-level documentation initiative, with evidence that people outside the candidate's direct circle have adopted or contributed to it unprompted (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | Adopted org-wide with measurable improvement in documentation quality/coverage, sustained independent of the candidate's ongoing involvement |

**Note:** per the standard's Evidence Guide critical aspect (`../../standard/2-communication.md`), culture-fostering claims (P5+) are evidenced by other people's communication or documentation habits changing, not just the practitioner's own output — an example limited to the candidate's own norm-setting or documentation effort, with no instance of anyone else adopting it, does not pass the culture-fostering rows in this table (2.5, 2.7, 2.9, 2.13, 2.14, 2.15), and does not clear the corresponding badge in `../badges.md`. Rows 2.4, 2.6, and 2.8 assess personal communication effectiveness, not culture-fostering, and are unaffected by this note.

**Note on evidence availability:** not every candidate's engagement includes direct client contact or a cross-timezone counterpart. Where the candidate's project has no client-facing or distributed-team dimension, mark the client-facing and cross-timezone rows not applicable per `../badges.md` §1.7 and weight the remaining rows accordingly — do not fail a candidate solely for lacking access to client-facing work. Where the candidate's day-to-day counterpart is a client PM/tech lead rather than an internal peer, client-facing and async evidence may substitute for "diverse team" evidence in rows 2.4/2.6, since the client relationship is itself the diverse audience at this level.

## Submission format

- Link to the communication artifact, norm, or documentation initiative
- 1-paragraph context: what existed before, what changed, and how it's measured or known to have worked
- For client-facing evidence: note whether the audience was internal or an external client stakeholder, and (if external) how tone/escalation were calibrated for the client relationship
- For async/cross-timezone evidence: note the timezone gap involved and what the handoff assumed the receiving side would need without a live call
- For P6/P7: name the teams/company-wide (or client engagement-wide) audience involved and any resistance encountered; adoption claims must include the `../badges.md` §1.6 3-part artifact (named contact, dated reference, what changed)

## Review process

- Reviewed by a P6+ engineer not on the candidate's team, plus the candidate's manager
- 30-minute review session
- Questions to ask: "How do you know this actually improved communication or documentation, versus just adding more of it?" "What would you do differently if you started this again?"
- For client-facing evidence, also ask: "How did you decide what to escalate internally versus raise directly with the client?" "What would a US enterprise client's technical leadership have pushed back on in this artifact, if anything?"
- Where the candidate's evidence is real client correspondence, screenshots/redacted excerpts are acceptable in place of a live link, provided client-confidential details are removed, or use the `../badges.md` §1.5 in-place attestation path if redaction/export isn't contractually possible
- **P7 only:** add a company-wide communication or teaching demonstration — candidate presents to a mixed audience outside their usual circle; reviewer observes for clarity and audience adaptation at scale
- Where the row also serves as badge evidence (`FCC-2-P5`/`P6`/`P7`), the reviewer's sign-off and date, plus (at P6) the delivery/account manager co-signer per `../badges.md` §1.1, are recorded against the badge as well as the portfolio record
