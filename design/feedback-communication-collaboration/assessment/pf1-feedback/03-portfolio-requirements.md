# PF 1 — Feedback: Portfolio Requirements

**Redesign note (2026-08-03):** each row below is also the named evidence source for a P5/P6/P7 badge in `../badges.md` (`FCC-1-P5`, `FCC-1-P6`, `FCC-1-P7`). A badge is not awarded from this table alone — the reviewer sign-off and, at P4/P6-equivalent culture claims, the adoption-evidence artifact (badges.md §1.6) are also required.

**Used when:** promotion review to P5/P6/P7, or lead/principal/architect-level hiring.

## Required evidence items

| Item | Maps to PC | Quality bar: sufficient | Quality bar: good |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence of fostering a feedback-giving culture on their team | 1.4 [P5] | At least one instance of a teammate visibly adopting the behavior — giving feedback unprompted, the way the candidate models it — not just the candidate's own modeling (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | The habit has spread beyond a single instance — multiple teammates giving feedback unprompted, or a recurring team ritual (retro format, peer review norm) the candidate introduced or strengthened |
| Coaching teammates on matrixed feedback (client-directed day-to-day vs. LFT formal record) | 1.5a [P5] | At least one documented instance of the candidate helping a teammate separate feedback from a client stakeholder (day-to-day direction) from the formal LFT evaluation record, and surfacing the client-side input to the teammate's LFT manager | A recurring practice on the team — e.g. a norm for logging client feedback so it reaches the LFT manager, adopted by more than one teammate independent of the candidate prompting it each time |
| Evidence of fostering a feedback-seeking culture on their team | 1.8 [P5] | At least one instance of a teammate visibly adopting feedback-seeking as a result of the candidate's modeling — not just the candidate's own habit (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | A recurring team ritual exists (retro format, peer review norm) that the candidate introduced or strengthened, now followed by the team independent of the candidate |
| Evidence of fostering a feedback-giving culture across several teams | 1.5 [P6] | One cross-team example of someone other than the candidate visibly adopting the behavior — not just the candidate's own cross-team activity (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | A recurring pattern adopted by more than one team, independent of the candidate's direct involvement |
| Evidence of fostering a feedback-seeking culture across several teams and stakeholders | 1.9 [P6] | One cross-team example involving a business stakeholder — for many candidates this will be a client lead or client PM across accounts rather than an internal-only stakeholder — where someone other than the candidate visibly adopted the feedback-seeking practice (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | A recurring practice, with stakeholders (including client-side ones) included as active participants, not just recipients |
| (P7) Evidence of fostering a feedback-giving culture across the organization | 1.6 [P7] | One org-level example of someone outside the candidate's direct circle visibly adopting the behavior (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | The practice is cited elsewhere in the org as something the candidate helped establish, adopted independent of the candidate's direct involvement |
| (P7) Evidence of fostering a feedback-seeking culture across the organization | 1.10 [P7] | One org-level example of someone other than the candidate visibly adopting the feedback-seeking practice (self-modeling alone does not meet this bar) | The practice is visibly embedded in how multiple teams operate, not just modeled by the candidate |

**Note:** per the standard's Evidence Guide critical aspect (`../../standard/1-feedback.md`), culture-fostering claims (P5+) are evidenced by other people's behavior changing, not just the practitioner's own modeling — an example limited to the candidate modeling the behavior themselves, with no instance of anyone else adopting it, does not pass any row in this table, and does not clear the corresponding badge in `../badges.md`.

## Submission format

- Link to any documentation of the ritual, norm, or practice introduced (a retro template, a feedback guide, a written norm) — client-facing artifacts count where internal-only ones aren't available: a client status-update thread showing feedback given/incorporated, a cross-timezone async handoff doc, an escalation email trail, or a note confirming client feedback was relayed to the candidate's LFT manager
- 1-paragraph context: what existed before, what changed, and how you know it changed
- For P6/P7: name the teams involved (and, where applicable, client-side stakeholders or accounts) and any resistance encountered along the way; adoption claims must include the §1.6 3-part artifact (named contact, dated reference, what changed)

## Review process

- Reviewed by a P6+ engineer not on the candidate's team, plus the candidate's manager
- 30-minute review session: reviewer reads beforehand, session is for probing questions
- Questions to ask: "How do you know this actually changed behavior, not just that you introduced it?" "What pushback did you get, and how did you handle it?"
- **P7 only:** add a teaching/culture demonstration — candidate presents the feedback practice they've built to a group outside their usual circle; reviewer observes for clarity and evidence of adoption beyond the candidate's own network
- Where the row also serves as badge evidence (`FCC-1-P5`/`P6`/`P7`), the reviewer's sign-off and date, plus (at P6) the delivery/account manager co-signer per `../badges.md` §1.1, are recorded against the badge as well as the portfolio record
