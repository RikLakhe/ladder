# Feedback, Communication & Collaboration — Communication

**Competency:** Feedback, Communication & Collaboration
**Primary Function:** 2 of 3 — Communication
**Domain classification:** Core
**Version date:** 2026-07-06

## Scope

Communicating clearly to a widening audience, and sharing knowledge and contributing to documentation — from communicating clearly in written and verbal form at P2, up to fostering a communication and knowledge-sharing culture across the organization at P7. Applies P2–P7. Given LFT's delivery model, "audience-oriented" communication routinely includes client stakeholders (a different company, often a different culture and geography) as well as internal teammates, and distributed, cross-timezone working (Nepal/India delivering to primarily US-based clients) is the default working condition, not an edge case. This PF distinguishes internal team communication from client-facing communication (tone, escalation protocol, expectation management) and treats asynchronous, cross-timezone communication as a baseline skill. Excludes the feedback-specific behaviors covered in PF 1, and the collaboration-specific behaviors covered in PF 3.

## Performance Criteria by Level

**P2**
- A practitioner at this level can: communicate clearly, concisely, and in an audience-oriented way in written and verbal form, actively listening to others and attending to nonverbal communication
- A practitioner at this level can: share knowledge of their area of work frequently with teammates
- A practitioner at this level can: communicate asynchronously in writing (status updates, handoff notes) in a way that a teammate or client contact in a different time zone can act on without needing a same-day reply

**P3**
- A practitioner at this level can: communicate clearly, concisely, and in an audience-oriented way in written and verbal form, usually, on both technical and non-technical subjects
- A practitioner at this level can: contribute to their team's documentation, watching for opportunities to share knowledge beyond routine sharing
- A practitioner at this level can: communicate directly with client stakeholders on routine matters (status, clarifying questions) in a tone appropriate to a client relationship, distinct from informal internal team communication

**P4**
- A practitioner at this level can: communicate clearly, concisely, and in an audience-oriented way in written and verbal form, consistently, on both technical and non-technical subjects
- A practitioner at this level can: encourage others to share knowledge and contribute to documentation
- A practitioner at this level can: manage client expectations proactively (setting realistic timelines, surfacing risk early) and escalate client-facing issues through the appropriate internal channel rather than absorbing or hiding them
- A practitioner at this level can: adapt communication cadence and medium to a distributed, cross-timezone team, minimizing dependence on real-time overlap with client or teammate hours

**P5**
- A practitioner at this level can: communicate effectively with a diverse team
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of clear, concise, audience-oriented communication on their team
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of documentation and knowledge sharing within their team and with the team's business stakeholders
- A practitioner at this level can: set the escalation protocol and tone standard for client-facing communication on their team, and coach teammates on managing client expectations across the relationship
- A practitioner at this level can: design team communication norms (handoff notes, overlap-hour meeting discipline, async-first defaults) that hold up across a cross-timezone, client-facing delivery model

**P6**
- A practitioner at this level can: communicate effectively with a diverse set of teams
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of effective communication across several teams
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of documentation and knowledge sharing across several teams

**P7**
- A practitioner at this level can: communicate effectively across the company
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of effective communication across the company
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of documentation and knowledge sharing across the organization

## Required Knowledge

1. **Audience-oriented communication principles** (adapting message, medium, and depth to who's receiving it) — the throughline connecting every level of the Effective Communication row, from "audience-oriented" at P2 to communicating with a whole company at P7.
2. **Active listening and nonverbal communication basics** — named explicitly at P2 as part of the baseline; without it, "communicates effectively" becomes one-directional broadcasting rather than a real exchange.
3. **Documentation as knowledge infrastructure** (why writing things down scales knowledge beyond who happens to be in the room) — needed to make "contributes to documentation" more than a compliance checkbox.
4. **Culture-building mechanics** (how a personal habit becomes a team, cross-team, or organizational norm — rituals, modeling, incentives) — shared with PF 1's Required Knowledge item 4; P5–P7 PCs in this PF are entirely about fostering communication/documentation as a culture, not just personally communicating well.
5. **Cross-team and organizational communication design** (channels, forums, and cadences that work at scale) — needed specifically for P6/P7, where "communicating effectively" no longer means one conversation but a whole communication structure.
6. **Client vs. internal communication protocols** (tone calibration, escalation paths, expectation management across a client relationship where the client is a different company, often with a different culture) — needed from P3 onward, where "audience-oriented" communication starts including client stakeholders and the stakes of mishandling tone or escalation are commercial, not just interpersonal.
7. **Distributed and cross-timezone communication practices** (async-first writing, structured handoff notes, deliberate overlap-hour scheduling) — needed as a baseline skill given Nepal/India-to-US delivery is the default working condition, not an exception; without it, "communicates effectively" silently assumes co-location.

## Required Skills

- Clear written and verbal communication, adapted to technical and non-technical audiences
- Active listening and reading nonverbal cues
- Writing and maintaining team documentation
- Coaching others' documentation and communication habits (P4+)
- Designing communication or documentation norms for a team, several teams, or an organization (P5+)
- Calibrating tone and escalation path for client-facing vs. internal communication (P3+)
- Writing and working async-first across a multi-hour timezone gap (P2+)

## Evidence Guide

**Critical aspects (non-negotiable):**
- Communication is demonstrably adapted to its audience, not a one-size-fits-all style
- Knowledge sharing produces a durable artifact (documentation) at P3+, not just verbal transmission that disappears when the conversation ends
- Culture-fostering claims (P5+) are evidenced by other people's communication or documentation habits changing, not just the practitioner's own output
- Client-facing communication (P3+) is evidenced separately from internal team communication — a client email/call/status update, not just an internal Slack message — and shows a tone and escalation choice appropriate to a client relationship, not the same register used with teammates
- Async/cross-timezone competence is evidenced by a real handoff artifact that let someone in another timezone act without waiting for a live reply, not just a claim of "we communicate well across timezones"

**Assessment methods by level:**
- P2–P4: review of 2–3 real communication samples (a written doc, a technical explanation given to a non-technical audience, a documentation contribution), plus a short conversation about how the audience was considered
- P5–P6: portfolio (evidence of a communication or documentation culture forming) + an observed team or cross-team communication (a presentation, a facilitated discussion)
- P7: portfolio + a company-wide communication or teaching demonstration

**Work products that demonstrate this PF:** written documentation, design docs, team announcements or presentations, onboarding materials, cross-team communication artifacts (newsletters, forums, shared wikis), client status updates or emails, async handoff notes, escalation write-ups.

## Hiring Signals

- **P2:** can explain a technical concept from their own work clearly to the interviewer; describes how they share what they learn with teammates; describes a time they wrote a handoff note or status update for someone in a different time zone.
- **P3:** describes a piece of documentation they contributed to and why they thought it was worth writing down; describes a routine client interaction and how they adjusted tone versus talking to a teammate.
- **P4:** describes explaining a technical topic to a non-technical audience, and adjusting the explanation when it didn't land the first time; describes a time they managed a client's expectations on a slipping timeline or escalated a client-facing risk through the right channel.
- **P5:** describes fostering a documentation or communication habit on their team — a template introduced, a norm that stuck.
- **P6/P7:** not assessed via live scenario — see Evidence Guide (portfolio + teaching/communication demonstration).

## Promotion Criteria

- **P2 → P3:** from baseline clear communication and ad hoc knowledge sharing, to consistent (if not yet fully reliable) communication across technical and non-technical subjects, a first documentation contribution, and taking on direct routine communication with client stakeholders.
- **P3 → P4:** from "usually" communicating well, to consistently doing so, plus actively encouraging others to share knowledge and document, plus proactively managing client expectations and escalating client-facing risk rather than just handling routine client contact.
- **P4 → P5:** from personally communicating and documenting well, to fostering a culture of both on their team, communicating effectively with a diverse team, and setting the team's client-facing tone/escalation standard and cross-timezone communication norms.
- **P5 → P6:** from team-level communication and documentation culture, to fostering both across several teams.
- **P6 → P7:** from several teams, to fostering a communication and documentation culture across the whole company.
