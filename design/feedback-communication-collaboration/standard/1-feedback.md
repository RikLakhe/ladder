# Feedback, Communication & Collaboration — Feedback

**Competency:** Feedback, Communication & Collaboration
**Primary Function:** 1 of 3 — Feedback
**Domain classification:** Core
**Version date:** 2026-07-06

## Scope

Giving feedback usefully to a widening audience, and seeking and using feedback as a tool for growth — from explaining how to give feedback when asked at P2, up to fostering a feedback-seeking and feedback-giving culture across the organization at P7. Applies P2–P7. Includes navigating the matrixed reporting reality common to staff-aug and fixed-bid engagements, where a client stakeholder directs day-to-day work while the LFT lead/manager holds the formal feedback and evaluation record. Excludes the broader communication and collaboration behaviors covered in PF 2 (Communication) and PF 3 (Collaboration).

## Performance Criteria by Level

**P2**
- A practitioner at this level can: explain how to deliver praise and constructive feedback in a useful manner, when asked
- A practitioner at this level can: seek out feedback from teammates and manager, and apply it as a tool for growth

**P3**
- A practitioner at this level can: deliver praise and constructive feedback to their team, teammates, and manager, in a useful manner

**P4**
- A practitioner at this level can: deliver praise and constructive feedback to their team, teammates, manager, and team's business stakeholders, when opportunities arise
- A practitioner at this level can: seek and reconcile feedback from both their formal LFT manager and the client stakeholder directing their day-to-day work, when working in a matrixed reporting structure

**P5**
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of delivering praise and constructive feedback within their team and the team's business stakeholders, demonstrating these behaviors themselves
- A practitioner at this level can: foster, within their team and with its business stakeholders, a culture of seeking out feedback and using it for growth, demonstrating these behaviors themselves
- A practitioner at this level can: coach teammates on separating client-directed day-to-day feedback from the formal LFT performance record, and on surfacing client feedback to their LFT manager so it counts toward evaluation

**P6**
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of delivering praise and constructive feedback across several teams
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of seeking out and using feedback across several teams and with their business stakeholders

**P7**
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of delivering praise and constructive feedback across the organization
- A practitioner at this level can: foster a culture of seeking out and using feedback across the organization

## Required Knowledge

1. **Feedback frameworks** (e.g. situation-behavior-impact) — "delivering feedback in a useful manner" is vague without a concrete structure; a framework is what separates useful feedback from a vague compliment or complaint.
2. **Feedback-as-growth-tool psychology** (why seeking out critical input, not just praise, accelerates development) — needed to make "seeks feedback and uses it as a tool for growth" a real practice rather than a box-checking exercise.
3. **Audience-appropriate feedback calibration** (how feedback framing should shift for a peer vs. a manager vs. a business stakeholder) — directly needed for the P3→P4 delta, where the audience for feedback widens to include stakeholders outside engineering.
4. **Culture-building mechanics** (how a personal habit becomes a team or organizational norm — rituals, modeling, incentives) — P5–P7 PCs are entirely about fostering feedback as a culture, which is a different skill than personally giving good feedback.
5. **Matrixed feedback structures** (formal LFT manager as the record-of-truth for evaluation vs. client stakeholder as the day-to-day director of work) — needed for the P4 delta, where a practitioner in a staff-aug or embedded engagement must actively route client-given feedback back to their LFT manager rather than letting it evaporate outside the formal record, and must not mistake client direction for a performance evaluation or vice versa.

## Required Skills

- Giving specific, behavior-anchored feedback in real time and asynchronously (e.g. in PR comments)
- Receiving feedback without defensiveness and visibly acting on it
- Coaching others' feedback habits (P5+)
- Designing feedback rituals or norms for a team, several teams, or an organization (P6+)

## Evidence Guide

**Critical aspects (non-negotiable):**
- Feedback given is specific and tied to a concrete instance, not vague praise or vague criticism
- Feedback-seeking is a visible, recurring habit, not a one-off response to being asked
- Culture-fostering claims (P5+) are evidenced by other people's behavior changing, not just the practitioner's own modeling
- For engagements with matrixed reporting (P4+): feedback received from the client stakeholder is demonstrably surfaced to the LFT manager (a 1:1 note, a written summary), not treated as informal and lost — otherwise the formal record is blind to the majority of the practitioner's actual working relationship

**Assessment methods by level:**
- P2–P4: structured conversation about recent feedback given and received, cross-checked against 1–2 real documented instances (a PR comment, a 1:1 note, a written piece of feedback)
- P5–P6: portfolio (evidence of a feedback culture forming — e.g. teammates giving feedback unprompted, a ritual introduced) + a manager/peer observation
- P7: portfolio + a teaching/culture demonstration

**Work products that demonstrate this PF:** PR/code review comments, 1:1 notes, written feedback (peer review cycles, performance conversations), documentation of a feedback ritual or norm introduced.

## Hiring Signals

- **P2:** can explain what makes feedback useful versus not, even if they haven't given much of it yet; describes a time they asked for feedback.
- **P3:** describes a specific piece of feedback they gave a teammate or manager, and what made it land well.
- **P4:** describes feedback they gave to someone outside their immediate team (a stakeholder), and how they adapted it for that audience; if in a matrixed reporting structure, describes how they've made sure client-given feedback reached their LFT manager.
- **P5:** describes how they've fostered a feedback habit on their team — a ritual, a norm, an example of someone else picking it up.
- **P6/P7:** not assessed via live scenario — see Evidence Guide (portfolio + teaching/culture demonstration).

## Promotion Criteria

- **P2 → P3:** from explaining how to give useful feedback, to actually delivering it to teammates and manager.
- **P3 → P4:** from delivering feedback within the team, to delivering it to business stakeholders as opportunities arise, and — in matrixed engagements — actively reconciling client-directed feedback with the formal LFT manager record rather than treating the two as interchangeable.
- **P4 → P5:** from personally delivering and seeking feedback, to fostering a culture where the team and its stakeholders do the same, modeling it visibly.
- **P5 → P6:** from team-level feedback culture, to fostering it across several teams and their stakeholders.
- **P6 → P7:** from several teams, to fostering a feedback-giving and feedback-seeking culture across the whole organization.
