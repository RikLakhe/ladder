# Strategic Impact — Onboarding Track

For a new hire (or an existing engineer newly picking up this competency) who needs to get up to speed fast — handoff-ready for a Staff Manager/EM to run without central involvement, per `../../COMPANY-CONTEXT.md`'s L&D goals.

## Day 1

**Read:**
- `01-concept-notes.md` §1 (Client business model literacy) and §2 (Contract-economics literacy) — these two are non-negotiable before anything else, because every other section assumes you know what T&M vs. fixed-bid means and can ask "how does this client make money."
- `../standard/1-business-acumen-strategy.md`'s Scope section (top of file) and the P2 row of Performance Criteria — orients you to what this competency actually is (business/strategy/product judgment, framed around the *client's* business since LFT is a services company) and what's expected of you on day 1 specifically.

**Ask your manager or buddy, on day 1, not week 1:** "is our engagement with this client T&M or fixed-bid?" This is a factual question with a real answer you cannot look up, and it's foundational to §2's content landing correctly.

## Week 1

**Do:**
- Guided Exercise 1 (`02-guided-exercises.md`) — client-context narration. Pick a real reference point from your actual onboarding ramp-up (a ticket, an onboarding doc, a walkthrough someone gave you) rather than waiting for a "good enough" one — the exercise works fine on a small, early example.
- **Codebase/account task:** shadow one real client-facing conversation or planning meeting this week (a standup, a roadmap sync, a client call), even just as an observer. Afterward, write the two sentences Exercise 1 asks for, informed by what you just watched, not just what you read.

**By end of week 1, you should be able to:** describe what the client's product does for its users and what your team specifically delivers, in your own words, to a buddy, without notes.

## Month 1

**Deliver:**
- Complete Guided Exercises 1–2 (client-context narration, strategy translation + contract-type drill) for real.
- Start Guided Exercise 3 (product-suggestion & roadmap-feedback artifact) — this one runs against real ongoing work over 1–2 weeks, so starting it in month 1 (rather than waiting for a dedicated block) is the realistic path; it's fine if it isn't finished by day 30.
- Have a first real conversation with your manager framed as a mini-review against the P2/P3 rows of `../assessment/pf1-business-acumen-strategy/02-live-demo-checklist.md` — not a formal assessment yet, just a check that the muscle is forming.

**Real task, not a sandbox exercise:** by month 1, you should have at least one genuine, documented instance of connecting a piece of your actual work to a client business goal or LFT strategic priority — a ticket comment, a standup remark someone wrote down, a 1:1 note. This is the seed of the artifact SI-1-P3's badge will eventually require; it does not need to be badge-ready by month 1, just real and started.

## Buddy check (2-week checkin)

Questions a buddy should ask at the 2-week mark:

1. "Without looking anything up — what does our client's product actually do, and who uses it?" (Tests Exercise 1's retention, not just completion.)
2. "Is this engagement T&M or fixed-bid, and what's one thing that means for how you'd scope a piece of work?" (Tests concept notes §2 landed as a real, specific fact — not a vague impression.)
3. "Have you noticed anything in your first two weeks that seemed more complicated than it needed to be, or an opportunity the client might value that hasn't been raised?" (Early, low-pressure seed for the P4 simplification/growth-spotting habit — not expected to have a polished answer yet, just checking the noticing instinct is starting.)
4. "What's one LFT strategic priority you've heard mentioned, and do you know where it's actually written down?" (Checks whether `my-role.md` / `company-okr-2026.md` were actually read, versus strategy staying folklore.)

If the answers are vague or recited-sounding rather than specific, that's a signal to slow down and revisit concept notes §1–3 before pushing further into Exercise 2 — this competency's entire training arc depends on early habits being grounded in real, specific facts about the actual account, not generic business-speak.
