## Phase C — Three-Pass Critique (2026-07-02)

**Pass 1 (Compliance):** found and fixed 1 HIGH finding — two Product Thinking sub-functions (originally 1.18 and 1.20) each bundled two distinct behaviors joined by "and" (e.g., "participate in roadmap feedback... and identify an opportunity to simplify..."), violating Gate 1's ban on compound statements. The LFT matrix itself already treats these as separate sentences; split back into their own sub-functions (1.18/1.19 and 1.21/1.22), renumbering the rest of the Product Thinking row through 1.25. Standard doc updated to match. Zero other HIGH findings — no banned verbs found in any PC (a literal "understand"/"know" appears only in Required Knowledge rationale prose, which isn't subject to the PC grammar gate), all 25 sub-functions trace to exactly one PC, level tags match the evidence brief throughout, and P2's correct absence of a Strategic Work sub-function was preserved rather than forced.

**Pass 2 (Load):** progression logic checked coherent — scope widens task → team → cross-team → organization, and participation depth deepens recite → apply → participate/decide → lead/influence across all three rows in lockstep. No internal overlap between the Business Acumen (individual judgment) and Strategic Work (organizational participation) rows despite superficially similar language at some levels. Practice load (25 PCs in a single PF) is the largest single-PF count in the pipeline so far — accepted per the same reasoning as prior exceptions, and workable in practice since any one assessment only touches the PCs at a candidate's current level, not all 25 at once. Flagged in functional-analysis.md as a signal that the pipeline's original "≈4–10 sub-functions per PF" guideline may need revisiting given it has now been exceeded by every competency built so far.

**Pass 3 (Market):** cross-checked against `../industry-evidence/strategic-impact.md` — every standard PC traces to a cited evidence-brief bullet. No new findings; the brief's own open items carry forward unresolved (no job-posting evidence sourced this session; Kickstarter's Principal Engineer corroboration for P6 is directional, not level-for-level). The staffeng.com "Architect" vs. "Right Hand" naming-collision caveat has now been flagged in all three competencies grounded in this pipeline (Technical Skill, Delivery, Strategic Impact) — worth an explicit, one-time decision from the pipeline owner rather than a fourth repeated footnote next time.

**Outcome:** one real fix applied (the compound-PC split); otherwise clean.

## V2 Independent Review (2026-07-07)

Strategic Impact was built entirely under V1 (self-checked only). Run via `/review-competency "Strategic Impact" all phase`. RED findings were fixed inline below; YELLOW findings and judgment calls are surfaced here for theme-lead (Rikesh) decision, not auto-fixed.

**Phase C (standard-reviewer, quality pass):** no unassessable PCs, no missing level-pair transitions — this PF's promotion deltas were the cleanest checked so far in the pipeline, with every P-to-P step showing a genuine qualitative shift (recite → apply → participate/decide → lead/influence) rather than a frequency ramp. One notable flag, distinct from a pipeline defect: sub-function 1.1 [P2] ("describe how their team's domain fits into the company's **CI/CD marketplace**") is quoted verbatim from LFT's own internal competency matrix (`lft-engineering-competency-matrix.md` line 352) — not something this pipeline introduced or can fix — but `COMPANY-CONTEXT.md` describes LFT elsewhere only as "a software services/consulting engineering organization... delivering to enterprise clients," with no other reference anywhere in the repo to a "CI/CD marketplace" as LFT's actual market. This phrase turned out to be a real, practical problem downstream (see Phase D/E below), not just a curiosity — **flagged for Rikesh to verify against LFT's actual current positioning**, since it may be stale matrix language inherited from an unadapted template ladder. A minor 🟡: PC 1.16 [P3] ("explain how their product area fits into the overall business, **suggesting** an improvement to it when relevant") bundles two behaviors the same way 1.18/1.19 and 1.21/1.22 were already caught and split during the original Phase C Pass 1 — this one was missed in that pass. A 🟡 on PC 1.18's "sometimes" qualifier (roadmap feedback) — the now-familiar frequency-adverb pattern seen in every competency reviewed so far, here compounded by the assessment package quietly dropping the qualifier rather than operationalizing it.

**Phase D (assessment-reviewer, 3 files):** failed Pass 1 on two items, both fixed. The standard's Evidence Guide requires "P5–P6: portfolio + a collaboration/facilitation observation," but the package only built the P7 teaching-demo add-on, leaving P5/P6 candidates assessed on documents alone with no live-behavior cross-check — added a P5/P6 observation step mirroring the P7 addition. The rubric also had zero candidate-facing content (Rule 4) — added a "For the candidate" section. The CI/CD marketplace phrase surfaced concretely here too: the rubric repeats it verbatim as the P2 pass bar with no definition anywhere in the package, and the live-demo checklist quietly substitutes generic "market" language in its checkbox while providing no actual sample prompt for this PC at all — confirming even the assessment builder found the literal phrase unusable, without fixing the gap (kept as YELLOW here since it's an assessment-side operationalization issue, not a missing-coverage compliance break; the training-side version of the same problem was RED and is fixed below). Other YELLOW items not fixed: PC 1.16's "explain fit" half is untested (only its "suggest improvement" half is); two portfolio rows each bundle two distinct sub-functions (1.13+1.14, 1.21+1.22) without testing them separately; the rubric requires both roadmap-feedback AND simplification documented at P4 while the checklist accepts either one in conversation — an inconsistent pass bar between the two instruments for the same level; no aggregate pass-bar rule stated in either the checklist or portfolio file.

**Phase E (training-reviewer, 5 files):** Overall verdict NEEDS WORK on first read, now fixed on the RED items. The CI/CD marketplace gap was confirmed as a genuine Day-1 blocker: the phrase is Day-1 reading and the target of Week-1 Exercise 1, but appeared zero times anywhere in the training materials with any explanation — added an honest operationalizing note to concept notes §1 (without fabricating what LFT's "CI/CD marketplace" actually is, since that isn't stated anywhere in this repo) giving learners a practical, hedged way to answer the question, plus a Day-1 onboarding callout pointing to it, and flagged the underlying phrase for Rikesh to verify against the source matrix. Also fixed: Exercise 2 had an unreachable success check (a hypothetical manager judgment) with no step actually instructing the learner to get that review, unlike Exercises 1 and 4 in the same file — added the missing review step. YELLOW items and judgment calls not fixed (surfaced below):
- Autonomous Project 1 points a P4-target learner at PC 1.20, which is P5-tagged, with no "on-ramp, not a substitute" caveat that Project 2 carries for its own P5-tagged content.
- Autonomous Project 3 is labeled "P5/P6 stretch" but its cited sub-function range includes 1.13, 1.14, and 1.25 — all P7 — directly contradicting the learning path's own claim that there's no guided/autonomous content past P5/P6.
- Sub-functions 1.15 [P2] and 1.4 [P5] have no guided exercise or autonomous project at all, only passive concept-note reading — inconsistent with their sibling sub-functions at the same level, which do get practice.
- The guided-exercises file claims exercises are ordered "simplest to most complex" (numeric order), but the learning path actually sequences them 1→3→2→4 — the two files contradict each other about what "the order" is.
- Concept notes §1 and §5 each promise LFT-specific grounding (revenue drivers/cost structure; how decisions actually get made at LFT) but don't deliver a pointer to where a learner would find either — no such document was found elsewhere in the repo either.
- The 2-week buddy-check asks about product feedback given, but the exercise that produces that evidence isn't assigned until Month 1 per the same document's own schedule.

**Not yet actioned (open, for Rikesh's judgment):** all YELLOW items above, plus — most importantly — direct verification of whether "CI/CD marketplace" accurately reflects LFT's current business positioning, since it's quoted directly from the company's own source-of-truth matrix and this review surfaced it as a practical, not just cosmetic, problem for real P2 candidates and new hires.
