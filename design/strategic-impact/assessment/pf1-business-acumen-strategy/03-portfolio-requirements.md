# PF 1 — Business Acumen & Strategy: Portfolio Requirements

**Used when:** promotion review to P5/P6/P7, or lead/principal/architect-level hiring.

## Required evidence items

| Item | Maps to PC | Quality bar: sufficient | Quality bar: good |
|---|---|---|---|
| A real decision connecting domain, strategy, and market trends, including adjacent-team strategy awareness | 1.4 [P5] | Decision documented, with market/adjacent-team context mentioned | Decision doc shows the market/adjacent-strategy reasoning explicitly changed the outcome, not just decorated it |
| Evidence of collaboratively deciding team engineering work based on org strategy | 1.9 [P5] | One example of a team decision tied to org strategy | A recurring pattern, with the org-strategy link stated each time, not asserted after the fact |
| Evidence of contributing to organizational engineering strategy work | 1.10 [P5] | One documented instance of being invited into org strategy work | A substantive, attributable contribution — a specific idea or analysis that shaped the resulting strategy |
| A new client product feature evaluated or created in collaboration with the client's product team, as a technical advisor rather than the feature's owner | 1.20 [P5] | Feature shipped, candidate's advisory role documented | Feature's rationale, alternatives considered, contract-economics tradeoffs (scope/margin/utilization), and outcome are documented, with candidate's specific judgment calls identified |
| Regular participation in team/account roadmap creation, and design/product simplification through proactive conversations | 1.21, 1.22 [P5] | Roadmap contribution documented across more than one cycle | A visible pattern of proactively simplifying scope or design before being asked, with at least one concrete before/after example |
| A contract-aware technical recommendation — a documented case where a recommendation to the client explicitly weighed the engagement's contract type (T&M vs. fixed-bid), margin, or utilization impact, not just the technical merits | 1.4, 1.17 [P5] | Recommendation documented, contract-economics reasoning stated | Recommendation changed scope, pricing, or delivery approach as a direct result of the economics reasoning, with the outcome documented |
| An account-growth opportunity identified and acted on — an expansion, cross-sell of an adjacent LFT capability, or additional scope the client valued | new — extends 1.20/1.23 for services context [P5] | Opportunity documented and raised to the account lead/manager | Opportunity was pursued and resulted in a documented account outcome (expanded scope, new workstream, renewed confidence cited by the client) |
| A cross-team business decision applying multi-team (and, where applicable, multi-account) domain understanding | 1.5 [P6] | Decision documented, other team(s) confirm it was informed by real understanding of their domain | Decision demonstrably prevented a cross-team misunderstanding or missed dependency |
| A cross-team strategic effort they led | 1.11 [P6] | Effort documented, cross-team alignment reached | Alignment reached despite an initial disagreement, with the resolution path documented |
| Evidence of participating in a strategic organizational decision or plan | 1.12 [P6] | One documented instance of participation | A substantive, attributable contribution to the decision or plan, not just attendance |
| A recognized differentiator relative to alternatives the client was weighing (another vendor, in-house build, a different technical approach) — demonstrating trusted-advisor influence, not product ownership | 1.23 [P6] | Opportunity/differentiator documented and shared | Opportunity was acted upon, with a documented outcome (e.g. the client chose LFT's recommended approach, or expanded the engagement as a result) |
| A roadmap refined across teams based on technical strategy and constraints | 1.24 [P6] | Roadmap doc shows cross-team refinement | Multiple teams adopted the refined roadmap, with the technical rationale documented |
| A client advisory memo — a written recommendation to client technical leadership on a strategic technical direction, distinct from an internal engineering decision doc | new — extends 1.5/1.23 for services context [P6] | Memo documented, delivered to client counterpart | Client leadership cites the memo as having changed their direction, or it's referenced in a subsequent client planning artifact |
| An account-growth plan spanning one or more client accounts — cross-sell, expansion, or renewal strategy | new — extends 1.11/1.24 for services context [P6] | Plan documented for at least one account | Plan covers more than one account, or resulted in a documented expansion/renewal outcome |
| (P7) A strategic organizational decision or plan they led | 1.13, 1.14 [P7] | Decision/plan documented and led by the candidate | Org-wide alignment achieved and documented, including how disagreement was resolved |
| (P7) A domain-wide business decision applying whole-business understanding | 1.6 [P7] | Decision documented with business-wide context | Decision is cited elsewhere in the organization as informed by a genuinely business-wide view, not just their own domain's |
| (P7) A roadmap created or redefined across the organization with product and business counterparts | 1.25 [P7] | Roadmap documented and co-owned with product/business counterparts | Roadmap became the organization's reference plan, with named product/business co-owners confirming the collaboration |
| (P7) Strategic influence and account growth across a portfolio of several client accounts, not just one | new — extends 1.6/1.25 for services context [P7] | Influence/growth activity documented across at least two client accounts | Portfolio-level growth or strategic outcome documented, with named account leads/business counterparts confirming the candidate's cross-account role |
| (P7) A one-off, high-stakes strategic decision with a documented major business consequence for LFT or the client, or an account-level trust/credibility claim explicitly attributed to a named client executive — a genuinely P7-distinct services signal, distinct from the portfolio-spanning row above | new — extends 1.13/1.14 for services context [P7] | Decision or client-executive trust claim documented, backed by an independently-existing, dated artifact created at the time (e.g. a client escalation email, a signed change order, a contract amendment, a leadership decision memo, or a client reference letter/review document) — not solely a co-signer's after-the-fact retrospective account | The artifact demonstrates the decision determined whether a major engagement was retained, restructured, or lost, or the client-executive trust claim is corroborated by a second independent, dated source, with the business consequence explicitly stated |

## Submission format

- Link to the decision doc, roadmap, strategy document, feature spec, contract-aware recommendation, client advisory memo, or account-growth plan
- 1-paragraph context: the situation, who was involved (naming the client account, not just the internal team), why the approach taken won out over alternatives, and — where relevant — the contract type (T&M/fixed-bid) in play
- For P6/P7: name the teams, client account(s), product, and business counterparts involved, and any friction encountered along the way
- For the P7 one-off high-stakes decision / client-executive-trust row: include the independently-existing dated artifact itself (not a description of it), the date it was created, and — where a crisis-style event is the evidence offered — an explicit note that the same dated-artifact rule applies (a co-signer's retrospective account alone is not sufficient)

## Review process

- Reviewed by a P6+ engineer not on the candidate's team, plus the candidate's manager
- 30-minute review session: reviewer reads beforehand, session is for probing questions
- Questions to ask: "What alternative did you seriously consider and reject, and why?" "Who outside engineering — including on the client side — would tell this story differently, and why?" "Where does contract economics (scope, margin, utilization) show up in this story, if at all?"
- **P5/P6 only:** add a collaboration/facilitation observation — reviewer observes the candidate in a real or simulated roadmap/feature-evaluation discussion with the client's product team; checking for the collaborative behaviors the standard's PCs describe at this level: deciding on team engineering work based on org strategy collaboratively with the team, contributing to organizational strategy work when invited, evaluating or creating a feature in collaboration with the client's product team as an advisor, participating regularly in roadmap creation, and surfacing account-growth opportunities
- **P7 only:** add a strategy teaching/communication demonstration — candidate presents a strategic decision, roadmap, or account-growth plan to a mixed technical/non-technical group (including, where possible, a client-facing audience); reviewer observes for clarity and for evidence the framing actually changed how the audience thought about the tradeoff
- **P7 row on the one-off high-stakes decision / client-executive-trust signal specifically:** the reviewer must examine the independently-existing dated artifact directly, not a description of it or a co-signer's secondhand account
